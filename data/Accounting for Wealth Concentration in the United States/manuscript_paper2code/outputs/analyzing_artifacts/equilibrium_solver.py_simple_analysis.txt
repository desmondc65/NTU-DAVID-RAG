The `equilibrium_solver.py` module is the central orchestrator of the life cycle model, responsible for finding the prices and transfers that clear the capital, labor, and government budget markets.

### 1. Logic Analysis: General Equilibrium Flow

The solver identifies the stationary equilibrium where household decisions (given prices) aggregate to the values that generated those prices, and the government budget is balanced.

#### A. The Firm and Production (Cobb-Douglas)
Based on the `model_parameters` in `config.yaml`:
*   **Production Function**: $Y = \Psi K^{\alpha} N^{1-\alpha}$, where $\Psi$ is `aggregate_tfp` (1.55) and $\alpha$ is `alpha` (0.33).
*   **Marginal Products**:
    *   Marginal Product of Labor (Wage): $w = \Psi (1-\alpha) (K/N)^{\alpha}$.
    *   Marginal Product of Capital: $R_{gross} = \Psi \alpha (N/K)^{1-\alpha}$.
*   **Net Return**: The annual net interest rate is $r_{annual} = R_{gross} - \delta$, where $\delta$ is `depreciation_annual` (0.045).
*   **5-Year Conversion**: Since the model period is 5 years, the effective interest rate for the household problem is $r_{5yr} = (1 + r_{annual})^5 - 1$.

#### B. The Government Budget
The government must balance:
*   **Revenue**:
    1.  **Income Tax**: Progressive tax on earnings and non-corporate capital income (capped at `income_tax_max_marginal`).
    2.  **Corporate Tax**: `corporate_tax_rate` (23.6%) applied to capital income above a threshold $d_c$.
    3.  **Sales Tax**: `sales_tax_rate` (5%) on aggregate consumption.
    4.  **Estate Tax**: `estate_tax_rate` (20%) on the top `estate_tax_threshold_top_pct` (2%) of bequests.
*   **Outlays**:
    1.  **Exogenous Expenditures ($G$)**: 11.6% of GDP ($Y$).
    2.  **Transfers ($Tr$)**: Lump-sum transfers (2.7% of GDP used as a calibration target).
    3.  **Pensions**: Benefits $b(z)$ paid to retirees (Periods 10-16).
*   **Balance Condition**: $\text{Taxes} = G + \text{Pensions} + Tr$. In GE, we typically guess $Tr$ and solve for the price/quantity vector that satisfies this balance.

### 2. Implementation of `EquilibriumSolver` Functions

#### `compute_excess_demand(guess)`
This internal function is used by a root-finder (e.g., `scipy.optimize.root`) to find the fixed point.
1.  **Unpack Guess**: Receive a vector containing guesses for $[K, N, Tr]$.
2.  **Update Prices**: Calculate $w$ and $r$ using the `Economy.firm_production` logic.
3.  **Household Solver**: Call `HouseholdProblem.solve_lifecycle(w, r, tr)`. This performs the backward induction using EGM to obtain policy functions for $k'$ and $h$.
4.  **Simulation**: Call `Simulator.compute_stationary_distribution(policy_functions)`. This computes the distribution $\Gamma(s)$ and returns aggregate values: $K_{sim}$, $N_{sim}$, $C_{sim}$, and $TaxRevenue_{sim}$.
5.  **Budget Check**: Calculate the government budget residual using `Economy.government_budget`.
6.  **Calculate Residuals**:
    *   $Res_{K} = K_{guess} - K_{sim}$
    *   $Res_{N} = N_{guess} - N_{sim}$
    *   $Res_{Budget} = Revenue_{sim} - (G + Pensions + Tr)$
7.  **Return**: The vector $[Res_{K}, Res_{N}, Res_{Budget}]$.

#### `solve_ge()`
The main execution loop:
1.  **Initial Guesses**: Set initial $K$ and $N$ based on $K/Y=3.0$ and $N=0.35$ (targets from `calibration_targets`).
2.  **Price Vectoring**: Transform annual rates from `config.yaml` to the 5-year model scale.
3.  **Iteration**: Use a root-finding algorithm or a damped fixed-point iteration ($X_{new} = \lambda X_{sim} + (1-\lambda) X_{old}$). Damping (e.g., $\lambda=0.1$) is necessary for OLG models with elastic labor supply to ensure stability.
4.  **Convergence**: The loop terminates when the Euclidean norm of the residuals is below a tolerance (e.g., $1e-6$).
5.  **Finalize**: Return a dictionary containing the benchmark prices, aggregate quantities, and the stationary distribution $\Gamma(s)$ for post-processing in `ExperimentRunner`.

### 3. Alignment with Paper Methodology

*   **Market Clearing**: Consistent with Section III.C, ensuring aggregate capital equals household savings and aggregate labor equals efficiency-weighted hours.
*   **Pension System**: As per Section IV.E, benefits are linked to the last working period's labor productivity $z$. `EquilibriumSolver` ensures the `Simulator` correctly aggregates these payments.
*   **Corporate Tax**: The solver must handle the pre-household deduction of corporate taxes before individual capital income is taxed, matching the paper’s description of `tau_c` as a tax on a portion of capital earnings.
*   **Parameters**: Strictly pulls $\alpha, \Psi, \delta, \sigma_c, \sigma_l$ from the provided `config.yaml`.

### 4. Logic for "Economy" Helper (shared by Solver)

*   `firm_production(K, N)`: Implements the Cobb-Douglas logic and returns the tuple $(Y, w, r)$.
*   `government_budget(tax_rev, benefits)`: Takes aggregated results from the simulator and computes the surplus/deficit. It uses the 11.6% GDP expenditure rule for $G$.

### 5. Shared Knowledge and Constraints

*   **Grid Consistency**: The solver ensures the asset grid used in `HouseholdProblem` is the same one used for the integration in `Simulator`.
*   **Sequential Dependency**: The solver cannot compute the budget until the `Simulator` has calculated the full stationary distribution, as tax revenue depends on the joint distribution of $z, \kappa,$ and $k$.
*   **Efficiency**: `solve_ge` will be computationally expensive; the `HouseholdProblem` EGM step must be optimized (Numba) to allow the GE loop to converge in a reasonable time.
