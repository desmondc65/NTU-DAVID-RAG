The `experiments.py` module is responsible for executing the decomposition analysis described in Section VI of the paper. This involves systematically disabling specific model features—superearners, return heterogeneity, and bequest inequality—to quantify their marginal contributions to the US wealth distribution (Table 5 and Figure 7).

### 1. Logic Analysis: Counterfactual Scenarios

The decomposition analysis requires running the General Equilibrium (GE) solver under modified environments. Each counterfactual requires re-solving the model because changes in household behavior affect aggregate capital ($K$), aggregate labor ($N$), and the government budget (transfers $Tr$).

#### A. No Superearners (Scenario B)
*   **Logic:** As per Section VI, eliminate "superearners" by setting the productivity levels of the two extraordinary states ($z_7, z_8$) to the highest ordinary state level ($z_6$).
*   **Implementation:** 
    *   Modify the `z_grid` inside the `HouseholdProblem` instance. 
    *   Specifically: `z_grid[6] = z_grid[7] = z_grid[5]` (using 0-indexing for 8 states).
    *   Keep the transition matrix $\Pi_z$ unchanged to maintain the same population weights/flow into these states, but with reduced productivity.
    *   Re-solve the GE using `EquilibriumSolver.solve_ge()`.

#### B. Homogeneous Returns (Scenario C)
*   **Logic:** Equalize the rate of return for all households. The paper specifies setting $\kappa_i$ to its **value-weighted average** in the benchmark economy.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   Calculate the value-weighted average return ($r_{avg}$) from the Benchmark simulation: $r_{avg} = \frac{\int r \kappa(s) k(s) d\Gamma(s)}{\int k(s) d\Gamma(s)}$.
    *   Update the `return_grid` in `HouseholdProblem` such that all states equal $r_{avg}$.
    *   Re-solve the GE.

#### C. Equal Bequests (Scenario A)
*   **Logic:** Section VI describes "fully redistributing all bequests among recipients." Instead of bequests being correlated with parental $z$ and $\kappa$, they are distributed equally.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   In the `Simulator` module (used by `EquilibriumSolver`), modify the bequest distribution logic.
    *   Calculate total estates $E$. 
    *   Set the bequest received by every household ($\Phi'$) to be $E / \text{Population}$ (or weighted by age receipt probability $p_{\Phi,j}$ if age-specific equality is targeted).
    *   This removes the "perpetuation of wealth concentration across generations" (Section VI).
    *   Re-solve the GE.

### 2. Combinatorial Analysis (Marginal Effects)

To produce the "whiskers" and average effects in Figure 7, we must calculate the marginal effect of each component across all possible permutations of the other components.

*   **Factors:** $E$ (Earnings/Superearners), $R$ (Return Heterogeneity), $B$ (Bequest Inequality).
*   **Scenarios to run ($2^3 = 8$):**
    1.  Benchmark (Base)
    2.  No $E$
    3.  No $R$
    4.  No $B$
    5.  No $E$ & No $R$
    6.  No $E$ & No $B$
    7.  No $R$ & No $B$
    8.  No $E$ & No $R$ & No $B$

#### Marginal Effect Calculation (Example for Superearners $E$):
The marginal effect of $E$ is calculated as the change in a metric (e.g., Top 1% Wealth Share) in 4 contexts:
1.  Benchmark vs No $E$
2.  (No $R$) vs (No $R$ and No $E$)
3.  (No $B$) vs (No $B$ and No $E$)
4.  (No $R$, No $B$) vs (No $R$, No $B$, and No $E$)

*   **Bar Height:** Average of these 4 marginal differences.
*   **Whiskers:** Min and Max of these 4 marginal differences.

---

### 3. Metric Aggregation

For every scenario, `ExperimentRunner` must extract and store:
1.  **Wealth Gini:** Calculated from the stationary distribution in `Simulator`.
2.  **Top Wealth Shares:** Cumulative wealth held by the top 0.1% and 1% of the population.
3.  **Top Earnings Shares:** Cumulative earnings held by the top 0.1% and 1% (to verify the impact of removing $z_7, z_8$).
4.  **Factor Composition:** Labor share of income for the top 1% (to reproduce the identification analysis in Section VII).

---

### 4. Alignment with config.yaml and Design

*   **Parameters:** Uses `model_parameters` for equilibrium targets and `calibration_targets` for benchmark verification.
*   **Grid Modification:** The logic follows the "Data structures and interfaces" by using the `EquilibriumSolver` to trigger new GE cycles after `HouseholdProblem` parameters are mutated.
*   **Robustness (Section VII.B):** An additional experiment should be defined where the `EmpiricalProcessor` provides a modified labor share (attributing 100% of business income to capital), followed by a full recalibration and decomposition.

### 5. Program Call Flow for Experiments
1.  **Initialize:** `runner = ExperimentRunner(solved_benchmark_solver)`
2.  **Decompose:** `runner.calculate_marginal_effects()`
    *   Iterates through scenarios 2-8.
    *   For each: clone the `base_model`, apply parameter changes (e.g., set `z_grid`), call `solve_ge()`.
    *   Store results in a dictionary mapping scenario IDs to `Simulator.calculate_metrics()` output.
3.  **Process:** Compute averages, mins, and maxes for marginal impacts.
4.  **Export:** Format data for Table 5 and Figure 7.
