This logic analysis for `utils.py` focuses on providing the mathematical and algorithmic foundations for the helper functions required by the household solver and the empirical processor, strictly adhering to the methodology described in the research paper and the configurations in `config.yaml`.

### 1. Rouwenhorst Method for Labor Productivity
The paper approximates the ordinary labor productivity states ($z_1 \dots z_6$) using a transitory component $a$ and a permanent component $f$. The transitory component follows an AR(1) process.

*   **Inputs from `config.yaml`**: 
    *   `ar1_persistence_annual`: 0.97.
    *   `ordinary_states`: 6 (total), which translates to 3 transitory states per permanent component (as the paper describes a $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ for transitions).
    *   `period_length_years`: 5.
*   **Logic**:
    1.  **Persistence Adjustment**: Convert annual persistence ($\rho_{ann}$) to 5-year persistence: $\rho_{5yr} = (\rho_{ann})^5$.
    2.  **Standard Rouwenhorst Construction**: 
        *   Define $p = (1 + \rho_{5yr}) / 2$ and $q = p$.
        *   For $n=2$, the matrix $\Theta_2 = \begin{pmatrix} p & 1-p \\ 1-q & q \end{pmatrix}$.
        *   Recursively build $\Theta_n$ using the Rouwenhorst formula until $n=3$ (the number of transitory states $a_L, a_M, a_H$).
    3.  **State Scaling**: The paper sets $\sigma_a^2 = 0.4\sigma^2$. The width of the grid for $a$ is determined by $\psi = \sigma_a \sqrt{n-1}$.
    4.  **Combined States**: The function must return the full $6 \times 6$ transition matrix for ordinary states, combining the persistent components $\{f_L, f_H\}$ (which are fixed, transition probability 1 to self) and the transitory components $\{a_L, a_M, a_H\}$.

### 2. Labor Supply Optimization (Root-Finding)
In the household problem, agents choose hours $h \in [0, 1]$. The First-Order Condition (FOC) for labor supply must be solved given a level of consumption $c$ or a guess for $k'$.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Objective**: Solve for $h$ in the equation:
        $$ \theta h^{\sigma_l} = \frac{c^{-\sigma_c}}{1+\tau_s} \cdot \frac{\partial y_d}{\partial \text{earnings}} \cdot (w z \epsilon_j) $$
    *   **Implementation**:
        1.  **Marginal Tax Calculation**: The function `get_marginal_tax_rate` (based on the log-linear disposable income function in Section IV.E) is required. The paper notes a cap at $39.6\%$ (`income_tax_max_marginal`).
        2.  **Boundary Constraints**: Since $h \in [0, 1]$, if the LHS > RHS at $h=1$, then $h=1$. If the RHS is 0 (or negative), $h=0$.
        3.  **Root-Finder**: Use a Brent-style solver (like `scipy.optimize.brentq`) to find $h$ such that the residual of the FOC is zero.

### 3. Interpolation for Endogenous Grid Method (EGM)
The EGM requires mapping the endogenous asset grid back to a common exogenous grid.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Linear Interpolation**: Given the extreme skewness of the wealth distribution, linear interpolation is preferred for stability, especially at the high end of the grid.
    *   **Extrapolation**: For assets exceeding the grid maximum, the paper implies wealth accumulation can be very high. Linear extrapolation based on the last two points of the grid is necessary to avoid artificial caps on top wealth.
    *   **Multidimensional Interpolation**: Since the state space includes $\{j, z, \kappa\}$, the interpolation effectively happens on the asset dimension $k$ for each discrete state combination.

### 4. Non-Linear Grid Generation
To capture the behavior of households with zero assets (precautionary savings) and the "superearners" with massive assets, the asset grid must be non-linear.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Method**: Use a power-scaled grid or a log-spaced grid.
    *   **Formula**: $k_i = k_{min} + (k_{max} - k_{min}) \cdot \left( \frac{i}{N-1} \right)^\gamma$, where $\gamma > 1$ (typically 2 or 3) to cluster points near the borrowing constraint $k=0$.
    *   **Constraint**: $k_{max}$ must be high enough to encompass the top 0.1% of the wealth distribution (identified in the calibration targets as 13% of total wealth).

### 5. Tax and Transfer Functionality
Calculates disposable income $y_d$ and government variables.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Disposable Income ($y_d$)**: Implements the log-linear function: $y_d = \exp(\lambda_0 + \lambda_1 \ln(y_{taxable}))$.
    *   **Corporate Tax**: Apply `corporate_tax_rate` (0.236) to capital income $r \kappa k$ above the threshold $d_c$ (calibrated such that revenue is 2.5% of GDP).
    *   **Estate Tax**: Apply `estate_tax_rate` (0.20) to estates $k'$ in the top `estate_tax_threshold_top_pct` (0.02) of the distribution.

### 6. Survival Probabilities
Computes the vector $s(j)$ for $j=1 \dots 16$.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Function**: $s(j) = [1 + \exp(\omega_0 + \omega_1 j + \omega_2 j^2)]^{-1}$.
    *   **Parameters**: Sourced from Halliday et al. (2019) as mentioned in Section IV.A.
    *   **Integration**: Used in the household's discount factor ($\beta s(j)$) and in the Simulator to weight the population by age.

### 7. Markov Chain Stationary Distribution
Needed for the Simulator and for checking the labor productivity distribution.

*   **Logic**:
    *   **Method**: Find the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue 1 of the transition matrix $P^T$.
    *   **Application**: Used to determine the share of households in $z_7, z_8$ (extraordinary states) and $\kappa_{top}$ (top return state) to verify against targets like the 0.76% and 0.03% population shares mentioned in Section V.B.
