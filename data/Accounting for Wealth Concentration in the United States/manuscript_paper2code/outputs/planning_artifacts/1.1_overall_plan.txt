This plan outlines a roadmap to reproduce "Accounting for Wealth Concentration in the United States" by Kaymak, Leung, and Poschke. Since the paper relies on a complex overlapping-generations (OLG) model calibrated to empirical data, the reproduction is divided into data processing, model construction, calibration, and counterfactual analysis.

---

### Phase 1: Data Preparation (SCF & PSID)
The goal is to replicate the empirical moments used to discipline the model.

1.  **Source Material:** 
    *   Download Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF) waves from 2001 to 2019.
    *   Use the 2018 update of Piketty and Saez (2003) for IRS tax unit comparisons.
2.  **Variable Construction:**
    *   **Market Income:** Wages + Business/Farm Income + Interest/Dividends + Private Pensions + Capital Gains.
    *   **Labor Income:** Explicit wages + imputed business labor component.
    *   **Imputation Rule:** For active business owners without reported salary, allocate **75% of business income to labor** and **25% to capital** (based on the paper's regression coefficient $\alpha=0.25$).
3.  **Target Moment Calculation:**
    *   Calculate Gini coefficients for Net Worth (0.84), Income (0.66), and Earnings (0.64).
    *   Calculate top fractile shares (0.1%, 1%, 5%, 10%) for wealth and earnings.
    *   **Joint Distribution:** Calculate wealth shares held by top 1% income and earnings groups (Table 2).
    *   **Labor Share:** Calculate the labor share of total income for top groups (Figure 1).

---

### Phase 2: The Life Cycle Model Environment
The model is a 5-year period OLG model with incomplete markets.

1.  **Demographics:**
    *   Model 16 periods ($J=16$), ages 20–99.
    *   Retirement at $J_r = 10$ (age 65).
    *   Survival probabilities $s(j)$: Implement the logistic function from Halliday et al. (2019).
2.  **Labor Productivity ($z$):**
    *   Construct an 8-state Markov process.
    *   **States 1–6 (Ordinary):** Use a Rouwenhorst approximation for an AR(1) process (persistence 0.97 annually). Combine with a permanent component $f \in \{f_H, f_L\}$.
    *   **States 7–8 (Extraordinary):** "Superearners." Use transitions $\lambda_{in}, \lambda_{out}$ and relative levels $z_7, z_8$ from the paper's calibration.
3.  **Asset Returns ($\kappa$):**
    *   Implement 3 idiosyncratic return states: $\{r\kappa_L, r\kappa_H, r\kappa_{top}\}$.
    *   The transition matrix $\Pi_\kappa(z)$ is productivity-dependent (high-z agents are more likely to enter high-$\kappa$ states).
4.  **Bequest Motive:**
    *   Utility: $\phi(k) = \phi_1 [ (k + \phi_2)^{1-\sigma_b} - 1 ]$.
    *   **Note:** $\phi_2 > 0$ creates non-homotheticity (wealthy people leave proportionately more).

---

### Phase 3: Numerical Solution Strategy
Since there is no official code, you must implement the following:

1.  **State Space:** Define a grid for assets $k$ (densely packed at the bottom, very wide at the top to catch the 0.1%), labor states $z$ (8), and return states $\kappa$ (3).
2.  **Algorithm (Backward Induction):**
    *   Start at $j=J$. Solve for $c, k'$ using the bequest utility.
    *   Work backward to $j=1$. 
    *   For workers ($j < J_r$): Solve the intra-temporal labor-leisure choice $h$ and the inter-temporal consumption-savings choice.
3.  **Stationary Distribution:**
    *   Initialize a distribution of agents at $j=1$ (age 20) with zero wealth and productivity distribution from Table 3.
    *   Simulate forward using policy functions and Markov transitions to find the stationary distribution $\Gamma(s)$.
4.  **Market Clearing:**
    *   Find the equilibrium interest rate $r$ and wage $w$ such that the aggregate capital $K$ and labor $N$ match firm demand from the Cobb-Douglas production function.
    *   **Bequest Clearing:** Ensure the total volume of bequests received equals total estates left (net of tax).

---

### Phase 4: Calibration (Internal & Exogenous)
The model must be tuned to match the SCF moments from Phase 1.

1.  **Fixed Parameters:**
    *   $\sigma_c = 1.5$ (Risk aversion).
    *   Frisch Elasticity = 0.82 ($\sigma_l = 1.22$).
    *   Corporate tax $\tau_c = 23.6\%$, Sales tax $\tau_s = 5\%$.
2.  **Calibration Loop (Targeting):**
    *   **$\beta$ (Discount Factor):** Target $K/Y = 3.0$.
    *   **$\theta$ (Labor Disutility):** Target average hours $h = 0.35$.
    *   **Superearner Parameters ($z_7, z_8, \lambda_{in}, \dots$):** Target top 0.1% and 1% earnings shares.
    *   **Return Parameters ($\kappa_i, \pi_{in}$):** Target the top wealth shares and the relative rates of return by income group (Figure 2).
    *   **Bequest Parameters ($\phi_1, \phi_2, \gamma$):** Target bequest-to-wealth ratio and intergenerational wealth correlation (0.37).

---

### Phase 5: Counterfactual Experiments
This is the core of the paper’s findings. For each, resolve the model and compare to the Benchmark.

1.  **Experiment (a) - Equal Bequests:** Redistribute all estates equally across all recipients. Measure the drop in top wealth shares.
2.  **Experiment (b) - No Superearners:** Set $z_8 = z_7 = z_6$. This removes the "extraordinary productivity" channel.
3.  **Experiment (c) - Equal Returns:** Set all $\kappa_i = \bar{\kappa}$ (asset-weighted average).
4.  **Interaction Analysis:** 
    *   Perform permutations (e.g., remove returns AND bequests) to calculate the "Marginal Effects" shown in Figure 7.

---

### Phase 6: Robustness & Evaluation Metrics
1.  **Primary Metric:** Reduction in the Top 1% and Top 0.1% wealth shares (in percentage points).
2.  **Factor Composition Test:** Verify if the counterfactual models (like the "No Superearners" model) fail to match the empirical labor share (as discussed in Section VII.A).
3.  **Sensitivity:** Re-run the calibration assuming 0% of business income is labor (the "conservative" case in Section VII.B).

### Potential Implementation Challenges
*   **Grid Density:** To capture the top 0.1% of wealth, the asset grid requires a non-linear scale (e.g., log-spaced) reaching very high values.
*   **Superearner Transitions:** The probability of entering the superearner state ($\lambda_{in}$) is very small (0.76% of population). Standard simulation may require a very large number of agents (e.g., 500,000+) to stabilize the tail.
*   **Bequest Correlation:** The mechanism where children draw bequests from parents with similar $z$ and $\kappa$ requires careful indexing in the simulation code.
