[Logic Analysis]
- ['config.py', 'Defines the global parameters and structural constants of the model. Includes: demographics (J=16, Jr=10), preferences (sigma_c, sigma_l, beta), production parameters (alpha, delta), and the paths to SCF datasets. It acts as a single source of truth for the OLG engine and calibrator.']
- ['utils.py', "Contains numerical utility functions. 1) 'create_asset_grid': Generates a non-linear (log-spaced) grid to handle the skewed wealth distribution. 2) 'survival_probs': Implements the logistic function from Halliday et al. (2019). 3) 'tax_system': Implements the progressive income tax function, corporate tax, and estate tax logic. 4) 'rouwenhorst': Approximates the AR(1) process for ordinary labor productivity."]
- ['data_handler.py', "Handles empirical data processing. Classes: 'DataHandler'. Methods: 'load_scf_waves' to aggregate 2001-2019 data; 'impute_labor_income' to apply the 75% labor / 25% capital split for active business owners; 'get_moments' to calculate the Gini coefficients and top fractile shares (0.1%, 1%) for wealth, income, and earnings."]
- ['olg_engine.py', "The core solver for the household's problem. Class: 'OLGEngine'. Uses 'numba.jit' for performance. Logic: Implements backward induction from age J to 1. Workers solve for consumption, savings, and hours worked. Retirees solve for consumption and savings (with bequest motives). Inputs: Interest rate (r), wage (w), and tax parameters. Returns: Policy functions (optimal savings, consumption, and labor supply) for every point in the state space."]
- ['equilibrium_solver.py', "Solves for the stationary equilibrium. Class: 'EquilibriumSolver'. Logic: 1) Given policy functions, iterates on the distribution Gamma(s) until convergence. 2) Computes aggregate K (capital supply) and N (labor supply). 3) Uses a root-finder (e.g., scipy.optimize.brentq) to find the interest rate 'r' that clears the capital market."]
- ['calibrator.py', "Internal calibration routine. Class: 'Calibrator'. Uses global optimization (e.g., 'scipy.optimize.differential_evolution'). Logic: Iteratively adjusts parameters (beta, theta, superearner productivity levels z7/z8, return states kappa) to minimize the weighted squared error between model-generated moments and SCF empirical targets."]
- ['experiments.py', "Implements the decomposition analysis. Methods: 'run_no_superearners' (sets z7=z8=z6), 'run_equal_returns' (homogenizes kappa), and 'run_equal_bequests'. It re-runs the 'EquilibriumSolver' for each scenario to capture general equilibrium effects (changes in r and w)."]
- ['main.py', 'Entry point. Orchestrates the flow: 1) Loads configuration and SCF data. 2) Runs calibration. 3) Executes the suite of counterfactual experiments. 4) Generates tables and plots (reproducing Figure 7 and Table 5).']

[Shared Knowledge]
1. Numerical Speed: The OLG engine must use 'numba' or 'cython' for the Bellman step; otherwise, the nested loops (age x asset_grid x labor_states x return_states) will be too slow for calibration. 2. Asset Grid: The grid must extend to values several hundred times the average income to capture the top 0.1% of the wealth distribution. 3. Transition Matrix: The labor productivity matrix is 8x8 (6 ordinary, 2 extraordinary), and transitions to extraordinary states (lambda_in) are very rare but critical for the tail. 4. Accounting: Bequests received in the model must be balanced with estates left by deceased agents in the stationary distribution.
