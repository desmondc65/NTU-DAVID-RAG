The `main.py` script serves as the primary orchestrator for the entire computational pipeline. It manages the sequence from parameter initialization and benchmark equilibrium solving to the execution of counterfactual scenarios and the final generation of comparative metrics and visualizations.

### 1. Initialization and Parameter Setup
*   **Configuration Loading:** Load settings from `config.yaml` using the `Config` class. This includes demographic bounds (ages 25–99, 15 periods), preference parameters ($\sigma$, $\eta$), technology constants ($\alpha$, $\delta$), and government policy targets.
*   **Parameter Generation:**
    *   Instantiate `ParamsGenerator` to pre-calculate all exogenous components.
    *   **Labor Productivity:** Generate the $6 \times 6$ transition matrix and state values using the Rouwenhorst method (referenced from `productivity` settings in `config.yaml`).
    *   **Health Dynamics:** Prepare the 5-state health transition matrices and age-dependent depreciation rates ($\delta_{h,j}$).
    *   **Survival Probabilities:** Compute the $s_{j,g,h}$ matrix using the logistic function parameters ($\omega_0 \dots \omega_3$) to be used in the household's expected utility calculation.
    *   **Deterministic Wages:** Load gender-specific age profiles ($\epsilon_{g,j}$).

### 2. Solving the Benchmark Equilibrium
*   **Execution:** Call `EquilibriumSolver.solve_ge()` with initial guesses for the interest rate $r$ and the insurance premium rate $t$.
*   **Logic Flow:**
    1.  **Factor Prices:** Determine $w$ and $r$ based on the aggregate capital-labor ratio (FOC of the firm).
    2.  **Household VFI:** The `HouseholdSolver` performs backward induction from $j=15$ to $j=1$.
        *   *Retirement ($j \ge 9$):* Optimizes $c, k', m$ (medical spending) considering endogenous health and survival.
        *   *Working ($j < 9$):* Optimizes $c, n, k'$ considering progressive taxes $T(y) = y - \lambda y^{1-\tau}$.
    3.  **Simulation:** `SimulationEngine` runs 50,000 agents to find the stationary distribution and compute aggregate $K$ and $N$.
    4.  **Convergence:** The loop continues until the capital market clears and the government budget (Pensions + Medical Coverage + $G$) is balanced by tax and premium revenues.
*   **Storage:** Save the benchmark value functions ($V_{bench}$), policy functions, and simulation data ($S_{bench}$) for welfare and insurance comparisons.

### 3. Counterfactual Experiments
The script will sequentially re-solve the General Equilibrium for three distinct scenarios by modifying the `gov_policy` input to the `EquilibriumSolver`:

*   **Scenario 1: No Pension System**
    *   Override: Set replacement rate $\psi = 0$ and remove the pension component from the premium rate $t$.
    *   Mechanism: Forces households to rely purely on private precautionary savings for retirement.
*   **Scenario 2: No Health Insurance**
    *   Override: Set the copay rate $\phi_{copay} = 1.0$ (households pay 100% of $m$).
    *   Mechanism: Increases risk of wealth depletion due to medical shocks in old age.
*   **Scenario 3: Linear Tax System**
    *   Override: Set tax progressivity $\tau = 0$.
    *   Mechanism: Replaces the Benabou-Heathcote progressive structure with a flat tax. Requires solving for a new $\lambda$ to ensure revenue neutrality relative to the benchmark $G$.

### 4. Welfare and Insurance Analysis
For each scenario (Benchmark + 3 Counterfactuals), use the `WelfareAnalysis` class to compute:
*   **Compensating Variation (CV):**
    *   Calculate the percentage change in consumption required to make households (by age group: Young, Middle-aged, Retired; and by status: Single, Married) indifferent between the benchmark and the counterfactual.
    *   Formula: $V_{counter}(k + CV, \dots) = V_{bench}(k, \dots)$.
*   **Consumption Insurance Coefficients ($\chi$):**
    *   Perform a panel regression on simulated data: $\Delta \log c_{i,t} = b \Delta \log y_{i,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}$.
    *   Calculate $\chi = 1 - b$ to measure the pass-through of income and health shocks to consumption (replicating Section 6.2).

### 5. Data Export and Visualization
*   **Aggregate Comparison Table:** Consolidate results for Output, Capital, Labor, Consumption, and Interest Rates (matching Table 9 in the paper).
*   **Life Cycle Wealth Profile (Figure 6):** Plot normalized wealth accumulation across ages for the benchmark and counterfactuals to visualize the "precautionary saving" response.
*   **Medical Expenditure Share (Figure 7):** Plot medical spending as a share of wealth for retirees across different scenarios.
*   **Welfare Change Bar Charts (Figure 5):** Visualize CV across demographic groups to highlight who loses most without social insurance.

### 6. Program Call Summary (Sequence)
1.  `Main` $\rightarrow$ `ParamsGenerator`: Pre-calculate matrices.
2.  `Main` $\rightarrow$ `EquilibriumSolver`: Solve Benchmark GE.
3.  `Main` $\rightarrow$ `EquilibriumSolver (Scenario Parameters)`: Solve 3 GE Counterfactuals.
4.  `Main` $\rightarrow$ `WelfareAnalysis`: Compute CV and Pass-through for all results.
5.  `Main` $\rightarrow$ `Matplotlib/Pandas`: Generate final figures and tables.
