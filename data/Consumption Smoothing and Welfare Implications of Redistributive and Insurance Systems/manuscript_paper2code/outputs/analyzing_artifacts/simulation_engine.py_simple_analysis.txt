The `simulation_engine.py` is responsible for simulating the life-cycle paths of a large population of heterogeneous households (50,000 as per `config.yaml`) based on the policy functions solved in the `HouseholdSolver`. This module provides the aggregate supply of capital ($K$) and labor ($N$), which are essential for the General Equilibrium (GE) loop, and generates the panel data required for welfare and insurance analysis.

### 1. Initialization and Data Structures
The `SimulationEngine` class will store the results from `ParamsGenerator` and the policy functions from `HouseholdSolver`.

*   **Input Data:**
    *   **Policy Functions:** Multi-dimensional arrays for $k'(k, \tilde{e}, z, h, j)$, $n(k, \tilde{e}, z, j)$, and $m(k, \tilde{e}, h, j)$ for each household type (Single Male, Single Female, Married).
    *   **Transition Matrices:** Productivity matrix ($6 \times 6$) and Health transition matrices (5 states, age-dependent).
    *   **Deterministic Profiles:** Gender-specific wage efficiency $\epsilon_{g,j}$.
    *   **Configuration:** `num_agents`, `period_length`, and marriage matching probabilities from `config.yaml`.
*   **Agent State Tracking:** A structured array or DataFrame to track for each agent $i$ at each age $j$:
    *   `is_alive` (bool)
    *   `marital_status` (Single Male, Single Female, Married)
    *   `assets` ($k$)
    *   `productivity_index` ($z \in \{0 \dots 5\}$)
    *   `health_status` ($h \in \{0 \dots 4\}$)
    *   `average_earnings` ($\tilde{e}$)
    *   `labor_supply` ($n$) and `medical_spending` ($m$)

### 2. Initial Distribution Logic (Age $j=25$)
At the start of the simulation ($j=1$):
*   **Marital Status:** Assign households as Married or Single based on the fixed proportion $\phi$ described in Section 3.1.
*   **Education/Productivity:** For single agents, draw the permanent component $f \in \{f_L, f_H\}$ and transitory component $a \in \{a_L, a_M, a_H\}$ using the initial distribution in Table 4.
*   **Assortative Mating:** For married couples, use the matching matrix (Table 3) to assign education levels to both spouses, which determines their starting $z$ indices.
*   **Assets:** Start with $k=0$ (or a small bequest-derived value).
*   **Health:** Start all agents at the highest health status ($h=4$) or a distribution representative of 25-year-olds.

### 3. Simulation Loop Logic (Age $j=1$ to $15$)
The core simulation uses a forward induction process. For each period $j$:

#### A. Policy Function Interpolation
Since assets $k$ and average earnings $\tilde{e}$ are continuous, but policy functions are on grids, use **Linear Interpolation** to determine:
*   Optimal labor supply $n$ and next-period assets $k'$ (if $j < Jr$).
*   Optimal medical spending $m$ and next-period assets $k'$ (if $j \ge Jr$).

#### B. Transition of Stochastic States
*   **Productivity ($z$):** For survivors, draw the next $z'$ using the Markov transition matrix $\Pi$. For married couples, apply the intrafamily wage correlation $\omega = 0.1$ as defined in Section 4.3.
*   **Health ($h$):** For retired agents, the next health status $h'$ is drawn from the transition matrix $H_j$, where the probability distribution is shifted by the "new health capital" $I(m_j) = H m_j^\gamma$.
*   **Average Earnings ($\tilde{e}$):** Update the career average: $\tilde{e}' = [(j-1)\tilde{e} + \text{current\_earnings}]/j$.

#### C. Mortality and Marital Transitions
*   **Survival Check:** For retirees ($j \ge 9$), calculate the survival probability $s_{j,g,h}$ using the logistic function. Draw a uniform random variable $u \sim U(0,1)$. If $u > s_{j,g,h}$, the agent dies.
*   **Spousal Loss:** 
    *   If a married agent survives but their spouse dies, the household type changes to "Single" (Male or Female) for the next period. 
    *   The survivor inherits the total household assets $k'$.
*   **Bequest Collection:** If an individual or both members of a married couple die, their remaining assets $k'$ are added to a `bequest_pool`.

### 4. General Equilibrium Aggregate Calculations
After simulating all agents across all periods, the engine calculates macro aggregates:
*   **Aggregate Capital ($K$):** $\sum k_{i,j}$ across all alive agents.
*   **Aggregate Effective Labor ($N$):** $\sum (z_{i,j} \epsilon_{g,j} n_{i,j})$ for all working-age agents.
*   **Government Budget Components:**
    *   Total Pensions paid (based on $\tilde{e}$ and replacement rate $\psi$).
    *   Total Medical Insurance payouts (80% of $m_{i,j}$).
    *   Total Tax Revenue (Progressive income tax + 10% consumption tax).
    *   Total Premiums collected (Rate $t$ applied to labor income).
*   **Bequest Redistribution:** Calculate the total `bequest_pool` and determine the lump-sum transfer $Tr$ to be distributed to all working-age households in the next GE iteration.

### 5. Numerical Efficiency (Numba Implementation)
To handle the high-dimensional simulation efficiently:
*   **@njit Kernel:** The main simulation loop over `num_agents` and `num_periods` must be decorated with Numba's `@njit` to bypass Python's global interpreter lock.
*   **Vectorization:** Random draws for mortality and shocks should be pre-generated or generated using Numba-compatible random seeds.
*   **Memory Management:** Use a pre-allocated 2D or 3D NumPy array to store states to avoid slow dynamic list appending.

### 6. Summary of Key Variables from config.yaml
*   `demographics`: Use `num_periods` (15) and `retirement_age` (65/Period 9).
*   `health`: Use `gamma` and `h_productivity` to calculate $I(m)$.
*   `government`: Use `phi_copay` (0.2) to calculate the out-of-pocket medical costs in the budget constraint: $(1 + \tau_c)c + \phi m + k' = \dots$
*   `simulation`: Target `num_agents` (50,000).
