### Logic Analysis: `welfare_analysis.py`

The purpose of this module is to evaluate the economic impact of social insurance systems (pensions, health insurance, progressive taxation) on household welfare and their ability to smooth consumption. It operates on the output of the `EquilibriumSolver` (Value Functions) and `SimulationEngine` (Micro-data/Panel data).

#### 1. Welfare Analysis: Compensating Variation (CV)
According to Section 6.5, welfare changes are measured using **compensating variation**, defined as the percentage change in consumption required to make households indifferent between the benchmark and a counterfactual scenario.

*   **Logic for Aggregate Welfare (Utilitarian):**
    *   **Indifference Condition:** We solve for $\omega$ such that $V_{bench} = E [ \sum_{j=1}^{J} \beta^{j-1} u((1+\omega) c_{j, counter}, n_{j, counter}) ]$.
    *   **Utility Functional Form:** $u(c, n) = \log c - \theta_g \frac{n^{1+\sigma}}{1+\sigma} + v_g$ (Section 4.1).
    *   **Analytical Simplification for Log Utility:** Because utility is logarithmic in consumption, $\log((1+\omega)c) = \log(1+\omega) + \log(c)$. The welfare gain $\omega$ can be extracted:
        $$\omega = \exp\left( \frac{\bar{V}_{counter} - \bar{V}_{bench}}{\sum_{j=1}^{J} \beta^{j-1}} \right) - 1$$
        where $\bar{V}$ is the expected lifetime utility at age $j=1$ (weighted by the initial distribution of types).
    *   **Reference Configuration:** 
        *   `preferences.beta_annual` (converted to 5-year period $\beta = \beta_{annual}^5$).
        *   `preferences.sigma` (inverse Frisch elasticity).
        *   `preferences.eta` (equivalence scale for married).

*   **Logic for Group-Specific Welfare (Section 6.5):**
    *   Calculate CV for specific subgroups: Young (ages 25-44), Middle-aged (45-64), and Retirees (65-99).
    *   The paper notes that for state-specific CV, we compute the asset transfer equivalent and then convert it back to a consumption flow relative to benchmark mean consumption.
    *   **Implementation:**
        1.  Identify agents in the simulation belonging to the target group.
        2.  Compare their realized value functions $V(s)$ in counterfactual vs. benchmark.
        3.  Apply the inverse utility transformation to find the consumption-equivalent percentage.

#### 2. Consumption Insurance: Pass-through Coefficients
Section 6.2 defines consumption insurance as the ability of households to shield consumption from idiosyncratic shocks.

*   **The Empirical Model:**
    *   Regression: $\Delta \log(c_{i,t}) = b \Delta \log(y_{i,t}) + \epsilon_{i,t}$.
    *   **$\Delta c_{i,t}$:** Change in household $i$ log consumption between period $t-1$ and $t$.
    *   **$\Delta y_{i,t}$:** Change in household $i$ log earnings shock (or health shock).
    *   **Transmission Coefficient ($b$):** Measures the sensitivity. $b=0$ is perfect insurance; $b=1$ is no insurance.
    *   **Insurance Coefficient ($\chi$):** $\chi = 1 - b$.

*   **Shocks Analyzed:**
    1.  **Income Shocks (Working Age):** Pass-through from productivity shocks ($z$) to consumption.
    2.  **Health Shocks (Retirement Age):** Pass-through from health status ($h$) to consumption and medical expenses ($m$).
        *   Note: Section 6.4 specifically looks at $\Delta \text{log}(m)$ as the dependent variable to calculate the medical expense response.

*   **Data Handling:**
    *   Requires longitudinal data from `SimulationEngine`.
    *   Periods are 5 years (as per `demographics.period_length`).
    *   The analysis must be segmented by age groups (Young vs. Middle-aged) to replicate Figure 3.

#### 3. Data Structures and API Alignment

The class `WelfareAnalysis` will interact with the simulated `DataFrame` and value function `ndarrays`.

*   **`calculate_compensating_variation(V_bench, V_counter, weights)`**:
    *   Uses `weights` (the stationary distribution $\Gamma$) to compute the utilitarian welfare change.
    *   Distinguishes between `V_S` (Singles) and `V_M` (Married) using the `preferences.eta` scale.
    *   Result: A scalar (e.g., -0.037 for -3.7% in "No Pension" scenario).

*   **`estimate_pass_through(sim_data, shock_type='income')`**:
    *   Processes `sim_data` to compute first-differences for each individual $i$.
    *   Filters by `age` to separate working-age (income shocks) from retirement (health shocks).
    *   Utilizes `scikit-learn` `LinearRegression` or `numpy.cov/var` to extract $b$.
    *   Result: The coefficient $\chi = 1 - \frac{Cov(\Delta \log c, \Delta \log y)}{Var(\Delta \log y)}$.

#### 4. Reference to Config (config.yaml)
*   **Welfare scaling:** Use `demographics.num_periods` (15) for the summation of the discount factor.
*   **Equivalence scale:** Use `preferences.eta` (1.7) when calculating the utility of married households, as $U = u(c/\eta, n_m) + u(c/\eta, n_f)$.
*   **Evaluation Points:** Groupings for figures (Young, Middle, Retired) should be mapped from `demographics.start_age` and `demographics.retirement_age`.

#### 5. Integration Challenges
*   **Married Transition:** For married couples, the welfare calculation must account for the transition to "Single Survivor" state. The value function $V_M$ already incorporates the probability of spousal death ($s_{j,m,h} s_{j,f,h}$), so the comparison of $V_M$ at age $j=1$ is sufficient for lifetime welfare.
*   **Regression Robustness:** When calculating $\Delta \log(y)$, ensure that zero-income/zero-consumption periods (if any) are handled via a small epsilon or truncation to avoid $\log(0)$ errors, though the model's borrowing constraints and $v_g$ usually prevent this.
