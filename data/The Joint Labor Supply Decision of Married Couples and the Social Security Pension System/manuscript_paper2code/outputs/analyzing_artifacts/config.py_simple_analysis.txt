Based on the provided research paper, experimental design, and `config.yaml`, the following is the logic analysis for the `config.py` module.

### **1. Purpose and Scope**
The `config.py` module acts as the central repository for all exogenous parameters, model settings, and policy variables. It ensures that the overlapping-generations (OLG) model is initialized with the exact values calibrated to the 2013 U.S. economy, as described in the paper’s Table 1 and the accompanying text. It also handles the calculation of derived parameters (like the growth-adjusted discount factor) to maintain the balanced growth path.

### **2. Attribute Mapping from `config.yaml`**
The `Config` class will ingest settings from the YAML file and map them to class attributes.

#### **A. Demographic Parameters**
*   **Life Cycle:** Agents enter at age 21 and live to a maximum of 100.
    *   `age_start`: `age.start` (21)
    *   `age_end`: `age.end` (100)
    *   `age_retirement`: `age.retirement` (66)
    *   `total_periods`: `age.total_periods` (80)
*   **Population Dynamics:**
    *   `nu`: `production.nu` (0.009 - 0.9% population growth).
    *   `eta`: Specified in the paper (Section 3.1) as 0.667 (66.7% of individuals enter as married).

#### **B. Preference Parameters**
*   **Utility Function:** $u(c, l) = \frac{(c^\alpha l^{1-\alpha})^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}$.
    *   `gamma`: `preferences.gamma` (2.0)
    *   `alpha`: `preferences.alpha` (0.6530)
    *   `lambda_joint`: `preferences.lambda_joint` (0.6, the joint consumption factor $\lambda$).
*   **Time Preference:**
    *   `beta_unadjusted`: `preferences.discount_factor` (0.9827).
    *   **Logic:** Calculate the growth-adjusted discount factor $\hat{\beta} = \beta(1+\mu)^{\alpha(1-\gamma)}$ to ensure stationarity on the balanced growth path.

#### **C. Production Technology**
*   **Cobb-Douglas Function:** $F(K, L) = A K^\theta L^{1-\theta}$.
    *   `theta`: `production.theta` (0.375)
    *   `delta`: `production.delta` (0.075)
    *   `mu`: `production.mu` (0.015 - 1.5% labor-augmenting growth).
    *   `A_target`: `production.A_target` (0.8885).

#### **D. Earning Process (AR1)**
*   **Shock Parameters:** $\ln z_{j,i} = \rho \ln z_{j,i-1} + \epsilon_{j,i}$.
    *   `rho`: `earning_process.rho` (0.87)
    *   `sigma`: `earning_process.sigma` (0.39)
    *   `omega`: `earning_process.wage_corr_married` (0.29, intrafamily correlation).

#### **E. Discretization Grid Sizes**
*   `wealth_nodes`: `grid_sizes.a` (27)
*   `earning_nodes`: `grid_sizes.b` (15 nodes for $b_1$ and $b_2$ each).

### **3. Government Policy Parameters (Method: `get_policy_params`)**
This method will bundle policy-related constants into a dictionary for the `HouseholdSolver` and `SocialSecurity` modules.

#### **A. Social Security (OASI)**
*   **Taxation:** `tau_p`: `policy.tau_p` (0.1007, reflecting the 10.6% rate adjusted for employer-side deduction).
*   **PIA Formula:** Three-bracket progressive replacement.
    *   `brackets`: `policy.oasi_brackets` ([0.90, 0.32, 0.15]).
    *   `thresholds`: `policy.oasi_thresholds` (Normalized bend points).

#### **B. Individual Income Tax (Gouveia-Strauss)**
*   **Formula:** $\tau_I(y; m) = \phi_t \left[ y - (y^{-\phi_{m,1}} + \phi_{m,2})^{-1/\phi_{m,1}} \right]$.

    *   `phi_m1`, `phi_m2`: Placeholders from YAML; the logic should allow these to vary by marital status ($m=0$ for married, $m=1,2$ for single).
    *   `standard_deduction`: `policy.standard_deduction` (0.1 per adult).
