This logic analysis for `discretization.py` provides a detailed roadmap for implementing the state-space discretization required by the OLG model, ensuring alignment with the research paper's methodology and the settings in `config.yaml`.

### 1. Earning Ability Discretization (`rouwenhorst`)
The paper models idiosyncratic wage shocks as an AR(1) process: $\ln z_{j,i} = \rho \ln z_{j,i-1} + \epsilon_{j,i}$. To solve the household problem, this continuous process must be mapped to a discrete Markov chain.

*   **Input Parameters:** Reference `earning_process.rho` ($\rho=0.87$) and `earning_process.sigma` ($\sigma=0.39$) from `config.yaml`.
*   **Methodology:** Use the Rouwenhorst method (1995), which is superior to the Tauchen method for highly persistent processes ($\rho$ near 1).
*   **Node Generation:**
    *   Target `grid_sizes.e` (5 nodes).
    *   The nodes for $\ln z$ are symmetric and centered at 0.
    *   Calculate the standard deviation of the stationary distribution: $\sigma_z = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\rho^2}}$.
    *   The distance between nodes is determined by $\sigma_z$ and the number of nodes ($N=5$).
*   **Transition Matrix ($P_e$):** 
    *   Construct the recursive probability matrix $P_N$ starting from $P_2 = \begin{pmatrix} p & 1-p \\ 1-q & q \end{pmatrix}$, where $p=q = \frac{1+\rho}{2}$.
    *   The resulting $5 \times 5$ matrix represents the probability $\pi(e_{i+1} | e_i)$.
*   **Initial Distribution at Age 21:** 
    *   The paper specifies the variance at age 21 is lower: $V(\ln z_{j,21}) = 0.5 \sigma_z^2$.
    *   The initial distribution $\pi_{e,21}$ (the invariant distribution for the single-person state) is given in Section 3.4: $(0.0625, 0.2500, 0.3750, 0.2500, 0.0625)$.

### 2. State Space Grids (`create_grids`)
This function constructs the discrete coordinate system for the value functions.

*   **Wealth Grid ($a$):**
    *   **Size:** `grid_sizes.a` (27 nodes).
    *   **Range:** $[a_{min}, a_{max}]$. 
    *   **Logic:** Since households face a borrowing constraint that is tighter near retirement and wealth is highly skewed, use a **log-spaced grid** (or a power-spaced grid where $a_{i} = a_{min} + (a_{max}-a_{min}) \cdot (\frac{i-1}{n-1})^\phi$ with $\phi > 1$). This places more nodes near the borrowing constraint where the value function has the most curvature.
    *   **Constraint:** Reference Section 3.4 for $a_{min}(i)$. While the solver handles the age-dependent constraint, the grid should span the global minimum to a sufficiently large $a_{max}$ (e.g., 50-100 times the average income to ensure the boundary is not hit).
*   **Historical Earnings Grid ($b_1, b_2$):**
    *   **Size:** `grid_sizes.b` (15 nodes each).
    *   **Range:** $[0, b_{max}]$.
    *   **Logic:** These represent the Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME). $b_{max}$ should be set slightly above the maximum taxable earnings $\vartheta_{max}$ (referenced in `policy.tau_p` logic).
    *   **Spacing:** Linear spacing is generally sufficient for AIME, though nodes should be clustered around the Social Security "bend points" ($0.15$ and $0.90$ in normalized units from `policy.oasi_thresholds`).

### 3. Survival and Marital Transitions (`get_survival_probs`)
This logic populates the mortality risks and the resulting changes in marital status.

*   **Data Source:** Load the SSA 2010 Period Life Table for ages 21 to 100 (`age.start` to `age.end`).
*   **Variables:** 
    *   $\phi_{1,i}$: Conditional survival rate for men at age $i$.
    *   $\phi_{2,i}$: Conditional survival rate for women at age $i$.
*   **Marital Transition Matrix ($p_{m,i}$):**
    *   For a married household ($m=0$):
        *   Stays married ($m=0$): $\phi_{1,i} \cdot \phi_{2,i}$.
        *   Husband survives, wife dies (becomes single male $m=1$): $\phi_{1,i} \cdot (1-\phi_{2,i})$.
        *   Wife survives, husband dies (becomes single female $m=2$): $(1-\phi_{1,i}) \cdot \phi_{2,i}$.
    *   For single households ($m=1, 2$):
        *   Survival is simply $\phi_{1,i}$ or $\phi_{2,i}$. The model assumes no remarriage ($p_{m,i}(0|1) = 0$).

### 4. Joint Productivity Distribution (Intrafamily Correlation)
The earning abilities of husbands and wives in married households are correlated.

*   **Parameter:** Reference `earning_process.wage_corr_married` ($\omega=0.29$).
*   **Logic:**
    *   The joint probability of shocks for a married couple $\pi_{(e1,e2),i}$ is a weighted average of a perfectly correlated case and an independent case.
    *   **Formula:** $\pi_{(e1,e2),i} = \omega \cdot \text{diag}(\pi_{e1,i}) + (1-\omega)(\pi_{e1,i} \otimes \pi_{e2,i})$.
    *   This ensures that in $29\%$ of married households, the spouses share the same shock index, while in $71\%$, the shocks are independent.
    *   This joint distribution is critical for the `HouseholdSolver` when calculating the expected future value $E[v(s')]$.

### 5. Normalization and Scaling
*   **Productivity Growth:** All grid values (wealth $a$, earnings $b$) must be growth-adjusted using `production.mu` ($1.5\%$) and `production.nu` ($0.9\%$) as per the "Shared Knowledge" and Section 2.3.
*   **Wage Scaling:** Ensure the mean of the discretized shocks, when multiplied by the age-specific wage profiles ($\bar{e}_{j,i}$), is consistent with the normalization where the average male wage equals 1.0.

### Summary of `discretization.py` Requirements (Alignment with Design):
1.  **`class Discretizer`**:
    *   `rouwenhorst(rho, sigma, n)`: Implementation of the Markov chain.
    *   `create_wealth_grid(min_a, max_a, n)`: Non-linear spacing for the $27$ asset nodes.
    *   `create_aime_grid(max_b, n)`: Linear/bend-point-aware spacing for the $15$ history nodes.
    *   `get_joint_transition()`: Logic for the $5 \times 5$ (single) and $25 \times 25$ (married) transition probabilities.
    *   `load_survival_data()`: Dictionary or array of $\phi_{j,i}$ indexed by age.
