### Logic Analysis: `economy.py`

The primary objective of `economy.py` is to solve for the **Steady-State Recursive Competitive Equilibrium**. This involves finding a set of factor prices $(r, w)$ and government policy parameters $(\psi, tr_{LS}, \phi)$ such that households optimize, the representative firm maximizes profit, the government budget balances, and factor markets clear.

#### 1. State Space and Distribution Management
The economy consists of a distribution of households $x(s)$ where $s = (i, a, b_1, b_2, e_1, e_2, m)$. 
- **Grids:** Use `num_assets`, `num_earnings`, and `num_abilities` from `config.yaml`.
- **Growth Adjustment:** Aggregate variables are normalized by the population growth rate ($1 + \nu$) and the labor-augmenting productivity growth rate ($1 + \mu$). This ensures the model finds a stationary distribution on a balanced growth path.

#### 2. Forward Induction (Stationary Distribution)
Once the `HouseholdProblem` is solved for policy functions (specifically the asset saving rule $a'(s)$ and labor hours $h_1(s), h_2(s)$), we must compute the stationary population density $x(s)$.

*   **Initial Entry (Age 21):**
    - Households enter with zero assets ($a=0$) and zero work history ($b_1=0, b_2=0$).
    - The mass is distributed across marital statuses $m$ and earning abilities $(e_1, e_2)$.
    - Use `marriage_share_eta` from `config.yaml`:
        - Married ($m=0$): Mass = $\eta$. Earning ability follows the joint distribution $\pi(e_1, e_2)$.
        - Single Male ($m=1$): Mass = $1-\eta$. $e_1$ follows the marginal distribution.
        - Single Female ($m=2$): Mass = $1-\eta$. $e_2$ follows the marginal distribution.
*   **Transition Step (Age $i$ to $i+1$):**
    - For each state node, the policy rule $a'(s)$ indicates the next-period asset level. Since $a'$ is likely between grid nodes, use **linear interpolation weights** to distribute mass to adjacent nodes in the next age group.
    - **Marital Status Transition:** Apply survival rates $\phi_{1,i}$ and $\phi_{2,i}$ (from SSA tables).
        - $m=0 \to m=0$: Prob $\phi_{1,i} \phi_{2,i}$.
        - $m=0 \to m=1$ (Widower): Prob $\phi_{1,i} (1-\phi_{2,i})$.
        - $m=0 \to m=2$ (Widow): Prob $(1-\phi_{1,i}) \phi_{2,i}$.
    - **Ability Transition:** Use the Markov transition matrix (Rouwenhorst) for $e_1, e_2$.
    - **History Update:** $b_1'$ and $b_2'$ are updated based on current labor income and the law of motion described in the paper (approximate AIME).
*   **Normalization:** In each age step, divide the mass by $(1 + \nu)$ to account for population growth.

#### 3. Aggregation Logic
Using the stationary distribution $x(s)$, calculate aggregate quantities:
*   **Aggregate Assets ($W_P$):** Sum of $a$ weighted by $x(s)$.
*   **Aggregate Labor ($L$):** Sum of $(e_1 h_1 + e_2 h_2)$ weighted by $x(s)$, restricted to working age ($i < I_R$).
*   **Capital Stock ($K$):** $K = W_P + W_G$ (where $W_G$ is government net worth, usually assumed 0 or fixed in baseline).
*   **Accidental Bequests ($Q$):** Sum of assets $a'$ held by households who die at the end of period $i$, redistributed as $q$ to all working-age adults.

#### 4. Market Clearing and General Equilibrium (Root-Finding)
The `solve_equilibrium` method must solve a multi-dimensional root-finding problem where the errors are defined by market deviations.

*   **Inputs (Guesses):** 
    - Factor prices: $r$ (Interest Rate) and $w$ (Wage Rate).
    - Policy parameters: $\psi$ (SS Benefit Factor) and either $tr_{LS}$ (Lump-sum transfer) or $\phi$ (Income Tax scalar).
*   **Inner Loop (The Algorithm):**
    1.  Receive price/policy guesses.
    2.  Pass guesses to `HouseholdProblem.solve_backward_induction()`.
    3.  Receive policy rules; run `compute_stationary_distribution()`.
    4.  Calculate aggregate $K$ and $L$ from the distribution.
    5.  **Evaluate Errors:**
        - **Capital Market:** $Error_1 = |r - (\text{Marginal Product of Capital} - \delta)|$. 
            - MPK uses Cobb-Douglas: $\theta A (K/L)^{\theta-1}$ (from `firm_gov.py`).
        - **Labor Market:** $Error_2 = |w - (\text{Marginal Product of Labor})|$.
            - MPL: $(1-\theta) A (K/L)^\theta$ (from `firm_gov.py`).
        - **Social Security Budget:** $Error_3 = |T_{RSS}(\psi) - (T_P - T_{RO})|$.
            - $T_{RSS}$ is total benefits; $T_P$ is total payroll tax revenue.
        - **General Budget:** $Error_4 = |T_I + Q - (C_G + T_{RLS})|$.
            - $T_I$ is income tax revenue; $C_G$ is fixed gov consumption.
    6.  Use a non-linear solver (e.g., `scipy.optimize.root` with Broyden) to minimize these errors by adjusting the guesses.

#### 5. Alignment with Configuration (config.yaml)
- **Interest Rate Target:** In the baseline, $r$ should converge to 5% (0.05).
- **Capital-Output Ratio:** Adjust $\beta$ (via outer-outer loop if necessary) to ensure $K/Y = 3.0$.
- **Income Tax:** $\phi$ is adjusted to ensure $T_I / GDP = 0.10$.
- **Production:** Use `tfp_A`, `capital_share_theta`, and `depreciation_delta`.

#### 6. Numerical Considerations
- **Interpolation:** Since the state space is high-dimensional (5D nodes + Age), forward induction must use the same grid-logic as the backward induction to avoid "numerical drift."
- **Numba Optimization:** The forward induction loop (iterating over millions of state combinations) must be jitted.
- **Initial Guess:** The baseline economy assumes $\psi=1.0$ and $w=1.0$. These are good starting points for the root-finder.
