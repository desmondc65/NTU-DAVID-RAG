### Logic Analysis: `economy_simulator.py`

The `EconomySimulator` is responsible for simulating the cross-sectional distribution of households in the economy and aggregating individual behaviors into macroeconomic totals. It serves as the bridge between micro-level household decisions and macro-level general equilibrium conditions.

#### 1. Population Distribution Structure
*   **State Space Representation:** The distribution `x(s)` will be represented as a high-dimensional NumPy array. The dimensions correspond to: `[age, marital_status, wealth, aime_h, aime_w, shock_h, shock_w]`.
    *   `age`: 80 periods (21 to 100).
    *   `marital_status`: 3 (Married, Single Male, Single Female).
    *   `wealth`: 27 nodes (defined in `config.yaml`).
    *   `aime_h / aime_w`: 15 nodes each for husband and wife (historical earnings).
    *   `shock_h / shock_w`: 5 nodes each (discretized AR(1) processes).
*   **Initialization (Age 21):**
    *   According to the paper, households enter with zero assets and zero work history ($a=0, b_1=0, b_2=0$).
    *   Population density for Age 21:
        *   Married ($m=0$): Proportion $\eta = 0.667$.
        *   Single ($m=1, 2$): Proportion $1 - \eta$ each.
    *   Initial shocks ($e_1, e_2$) are distributed according to the invariant distribution of the Rouwenhorst process (or the specific Age 21 variance $V(\ln z_{j,21})$ mentioned in the paper).

#### 2. Forward Simulation Logic (`simulate_forward`)
This method iterates through the life cycle (Age 21 to 100) to compute the stationary distribution.

*   **Law of Motion:** For each state $s$ at age $i$, the simulator uses the policy functions (optimal savings $a'$, work hours $h_1, h_2$) to find the state $s'$ at age $i+1$.
*   **Mass Distribution (Interpolation):** Since $a'$, $b_1'$, and $b_2'$ are continuous but the distribution is on a grid, the probability mass must be distributed to the nearest grid nodes using linear weights (e.g., if $a_{grid}[j] < a' < a_{grid}[j+1]$, mass is split between indices $j$ and $j+1$).
*   **Marital Status Transitions:**
    *   Determined by survival rates $\phi_{1,i}$ (male) and $\phi_{2,i}$ (female) from the SSA life tables.
    *   Married ($m=0$) households can transition to Single Male ($m=1$) if the wife dies, or Single Female ($m=2$) if the husband dies.
    *   Probability $0 \to 0 = \phi_{1,i} \phi_{2,i}$.
    *   Probability $0 \to 1 = \phi_{1,i} (1 - \phi_{2,i})$.
    *   Probability $0 \to 2 = (1 - \phi_{1,i}) \phi_{2,i}$.
*   **Shock Transitions:** The distribution mass is multiplied by the Markov transition matrices $\pi$ for productivity shocks.
*   **Normalization:** The density for age $i+1$ is divided by $(1 + \nu)$ to account for population growth, ensuring the total growth-adjusted population remains stable in the steady state.

#### 3. Macroeconomic Aggregation (`aggregate_k_l`)
This method sums individual variables weighted by the distribution `x(s)`.

*   **Aggregate Private Wealth ($W_P$):** $\sum_{s} x(s) \cdot a$.
*   **Aggregate Labor Supply ($L$):** $\sum_{s} x(s) \cdot (e_1 h_1(s) + e_2 h_2(s))$. Labor is in efficiency units.
*   **Capital Stock ($K$):** $K = W_P + W_G$ (where $W_G$ is government net worth, usually assumed constant or zero in baseline).
*   **Output (GDP):** Calculated using the Cobb-Douglas production function: $Y = A K^\theta L^{1-\theta}$ (using `theta` and `A_target` from `config.yaml`).
*   **Factor Prices (Sanity Check):**
    *   Interest rate $r = \theta (K/L)^{\theta-1} - \delta$.
    *   Wage rate $w = (1-\theta) A (K/L)^\theta$.

#### 4. Fiscal and Social Security Calculations (`calculate_tax_revenue`)
*   **OASI Payroll Tax Revenue ($T_P$):** 
    *   Sum over all households: $\tau_p \cdot (\min(w e_1 h_1, \vartheta_{max}) + \min(w e_2 h_2, \vartheta_{max}))$.
*   **Individual Income Tax Revenue ($T_I$):**
    *   Apply the Gouveia-Strauss function $\tau_{I}(y; m)$ from the paper to the taxable income $y$ of each household.
    *   Taxable income includes interest income $r \cdot a$ and labor income $w \cdot e \cdot h$ minus standard deductions $\xi$ (referenced in `config.yaml` as `standard_deduction`).
*   **OASI Benefit Expenditure ($T_{R, SS}$):**
    *   Sum over retired households ($age \ge I_R$) the benefits calculated in `SocialSecurity.get_benefit`.
*   **Accidental Bequests ($q$):**
    *   Sum the wealth $a$ of all households that do not survive to the next period.
    *   The paper assumes these are distributed uniformly to all working-age adults. The simulator must calculate the total sum and update $q$ for the next iteration.

#### 5. Numerical Efficiency Considerations
*   **Vectorization:** Given the state space size ($~10^9$), standard Python loops are impossible. The simulation logic must be vectorized across the distribution array using NumPy or accelerated via `numba.jit`.
*   **Sparsity:** Many states (e.g., $a=max$ at age 21) will have zero probability mass. Operations should focus on the support of the distribution.
*   **Memory Management:** Pre-calculating indices and weights for the linear interpolation of $a', b_1', b_2'$ will significantly speed up the forward simulation.

#### 6. Transition Path Specifics
*   During the transition path, the population distribution is not stationary. The `simulate_forward` logic must be applied period-by-period, starting from the baseline stationary distribution $x_0(s)$, updating it to $x_1(s)$, $x_2(s)$, etc., using the time-dependent policy functions and factor prices.

#### 7. References to `config.yaml`
*   `nu` (Population growth) and `mu` (Productivity growth) are used for normalization.
*   `theta` (Capital share) and `delta` (Depreciation) are used for firm-side aggregation.
*   `tau_p` (Payroll tax) and `oasi_thresholds` are used for OASI budget balancing.
*   `age` parameters (`start`, `end`, `retirement`) define the loop boundaries for simulation and aggregation.
