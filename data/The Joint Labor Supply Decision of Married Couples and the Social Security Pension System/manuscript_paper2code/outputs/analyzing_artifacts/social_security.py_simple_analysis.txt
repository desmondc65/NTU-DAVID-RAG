### Logic Analysis: `social_security.py`

The `SocialSecurity` class (implementing the `OASISystem`) is responsible for calculating the Old-Age and Survivors Insurance (OASI) benefits, which are a central part of the paper's redistribution analysis. The logic must handle the standard benefit formula, spousal/survivors additions, and the marginal benefits required for the household's first-order conditions.

#### 1. Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) Calculation
The PIA is the base benefit derived from the Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME), represented in the model by the state variables $b_1$ (husband) and $b_2$ (wife).

*   **Formula (Section 2.1):**
    For a given AIME $b_j$ at age $i$:
    $$\psi(i, b_j) = \mathbb{1}_{\{i \ge I_R\}} \cdot (1 + \mu)^{60-i} \cdot \left[ 0.90 \cdot \min(b_j, \vartheta_1) + 0.32 \cdot \max(\min(b_j, \vartheta_2) - \vartheta_1, 0) + 0.15 \cdot \max(b_j - \vartheta_2, 0) \right]$$
*   **Key Constraints from `config.yaml`:**
    *   `age.retirement` ($I_R$): Benefits are zero if $i < 66$.
    *   `production.mu` ($\mu$): Productivity growth (1.5%) used to adjust thresholds relative to the indexing age (60).
    *   `policy.oasi_brackets`: `[0.90, 0.32, 0.15]`.
    *   `policy.oasi_thresholds`: $\vartheta_1 = 0.15$, $\vartheta_2 = 0.90$ (normalized bend points).

#### 2. Benefit Logic by Marital Status (`get_benefit`)
This method implements the OASI benefit function $\text{tr}_{SS,t}(i, b_1, b_2, m)$. It must support two modes: `baseline` (with spousal/survivors benefits) and `reform` (without them).

**A. Baseline Economy (Current Law):**
*   **Married ($m=0$):** The household receives the maximum of their combined own benefits or the spousal benefit top-up.
    $$\text{benefit} = \psi_t \cdot \max(\psi(i, b_1) + \psi(i, b_2), 1.5\psi(i, b_1), 1.5\psi(i, b_2))$$
    *This implies if one spouse's PIA is less than 50% of the other's, they receive 50% of the higher earner's PIA instead of their own.*
*   **Single/Widowed ($m=1, 2$):** The survivor receives the maximum of their own benefit or the deceased spouse's benefit.
    $$\text{benefit} = \psi_t \cdot \max(\psi(i, b_1), \psi(i, b_2))$$

**B. Reform Economy (Removed Benefits):**
*   **Married ($m=0$):** Households only receive the sum of their own old-age (OA) benefits.
    $$\text{benefit} = \psi_t \cdot (\psi(i, b_1) + \psi(i, b_2))$$
*   **Single/Widowed ($m=1, 2$):** Households only receive their own OA benefit.
    $$\text{benefit} = \psi_t \cdot \psi(i, b_j) \text{ where } j \in \{1, 2\} \text{ is the survivor.}$$

*Note: $\psi_t$ is the OASI benefit adjustment factor used to balance the budget.*

#### 3. Marginal Benefit Calculation (`get_marginal_benefit`)
The `HouseholdSolver` requires the partial derivatives of the benefit function with respect to $b_1$ and $b_2$ to solve the Kuhn-Tucker conditions.

*   **Marginal PIA ($\psi_b$):**
    The derivative of the piecewise linear PIA function:
    $$\psi_b(i, b_j) = \mathbb{1}_{\{i \ge I_R\}} (1 + \mu)^{60-i} \cdot \begin{cases} 0.90 & \text{if } b_j < \vartheta_1 \\ 0.32 & \text{if } \vartheta_1 \le b_j < \vartheta_2 \\ 0.15 & \text{if } b_j \ge \vartheta_2 \end{cases}$$

*   **Marginal Household Benefit (Baseline):**
    Based on Appendix A.1 and Figure 10:
    *   **For $m=0$ (Husband's side $\frac{\partial \text{tr}}{\partial b_1}$):**
        $$\frac{\partial \text{tr}}{\partial b_1} = \psi_t \cdot \left[ \mathbb{1}_{\{\psi(b_1) \ge 0.5\psi(b_2)\}} \cdot \psi_b(b_1) + \mathbb{1}_{\{\psi(b_1) > 2.0\psi(b_2)\}} \cdot 0.5\psi_b(b_1) \right]$$
    *   **For $m=1, 2$ (Survivor's side $\frac{\partial \text{tr}}{\partial b_j}$):**
        $$\frac{\partial \text{tr}}{\partial b_j} = \psi_t \cdot \mathbb{1}_{\{\psi(b_j) \ge \psi(b_{spouse})\}} \cdot \psi_b(b_j)$$

*   **Marginal Household Benefit (Reform):**
    *   Simple OA marginals: $\frac{\partial \text{tr}}{\partial b_j} = \psi_t \cdot \psi_b(b_j)$ for the individual worker, regardless of the spouse's earnings.

#### 4. Interface and Data Flow
*   **Inputs:**
    *   `state`: Tuple containing $(i, b_1, b_2, m)$.
    *   `psi_t`: The current year's adjustment factor (from `config` or the transition path).
    *   `policy_type`: String `baseline` or `reform`.
*   **Integration with `config.yaml`:**
    *   Always utilize `config.policy['oasi_thresholds']` and `config.policy['oasi_brackets']`.
    *   The `mu` parameter from `config.production['mu']` must be used for the age-indexing adjustment.
*   **Vectorization:**
    To optimize the `EconomySimulator`, the `get_benefit` and `calculate_pia` methods should ideally handle NumPy arrays for states $b_1$ and $b_2$.

#### 5. Logic for Transition Phasing (Section 4)
While the `TransitionSolver` handles the time-path, `SocialSecurity` must provide the specific functional forms for:
1.  **Fully Old Schedule (`tr0`):** Baseline benefits.
2.  **Fully New Schedule (`tr1`):** Reformed benefits.
3.  **Weighted Average:** During the transition, $tr = \omega \cdot tr0 + (1-\omega) \cdot tr1$ where $\omega$ is cohort-dependent. This logic should be callable by the solver to handle households aged 22-60 at the time of reform.
