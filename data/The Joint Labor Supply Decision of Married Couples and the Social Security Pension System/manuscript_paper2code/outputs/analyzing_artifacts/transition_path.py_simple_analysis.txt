The `transition_path.py` module is responsible for computing the dynamic transition of the economy from the initial baseline steady state to a new long-run equilibrium following the removal of Social Security spousal and survivors benefits. This process involves solving a fixed-point problem over a sequence of factor prices and government policy variables for $T=100$ years (as specified in `config.yaml`).

### 1. Policy Reform Logic (Section 4)

The core of the transition is the cohort-by-cohort phased-in removal of benefits. The module must implement the following logic for the Social Security benefit function $tr_{SS, t}(s)$:

*   **Schedule 0 (Old):** Current law with spousal and survivors benefits (as used in the baseline).
*   **Schedule 1 (New):** Only own-age (OA) benefits based on individual work history ($tr_{SS, t} = \psi_t (\psi(b_1) + \psi(b_2))$ for married).
*   **Phasing Weights:**
    *   **Immune Cohort:** Households age $\ge 61$ in year $t=1$. Their benefit schedule remains **Schedule 0**.
    *   **Fully Impacted Cohort:** Households age $\le 21$ in year $t=1$. Their benefit schedule is **Schedule 1**.
    *   **Transitioning Cohort:** Households age $i \in [22, 60]$ in year $t=1$. The benefit is a linear combination:
        $$tr_{SS, t} = \text{weight} \cdot tr^0_{SS, t} + (1 - \text{weight}) \cdot tr^1_{SS, t}$$
        Where weight depends on the household's birth year relative to the policy start.

### 2. Financing Assumptions (Section 4.2)

The module must support two alternative financing rules to balance the government budget (excluding Social Security):
*   **Run (a):** Adjust lump-sum transfers $tr_{LS, t}$ while keeping income tax parameters fixed.
*   **Run (b):** Adjust the marginal income tax parameter $\phi_t$ while keeping $tr_{LS, t}$ fixed at baseline.
*   **OASI Budget:** In both runs, the benefit adjustment factor $\psi_t$ must be adjusted annually so that $T_{RSS, t}(\psi_t) = T_{P, t}(\tau_{P, 0}) - T_{RO}$ (balancing total benefits against payroll tax revenue less baseline residual).

### 3. Transition Algorithm

The transition is solved by finding a consistent path for $\{r_t, w_t, q_t, \psi_t, \text{financing\_param}_t\}_{t=1}^T$.

#### A. Initialization
1.  Set $T = 100$ (from `config.yaml`).
2.  Import initial steady state ($SS_0$) and final steady state ($SS_{\infty}$).
3.  Guess initial paths for prices and policy variables, typically a linear interpolation between $SS_0$ and $SS_{\infty}$ for $t \in [1, T]$.

#### B. Iterative Loop (Outer Loop)
The loop continues until the paths of prices and aggregate variables converge within `solver.tolerance`.

1.  **Backward Induction (Time-Dependent Household Problem):**
    *   Solve the household problem starting from $t=T$ back to $t=1$.
    *   Unlike the steady state, the value function $V_t(s)$ depends on $V_{t+1}(s')$.
    *   For $t \ge T$, $V_t = V_{SS_{\infty}}$.
    *   For $t < T$, the `HouseholdSolver` computes optimal $h_{1,t}, h_{2,t}, a'_{t}$ given the prices and policy (including the specific phased-in benefit schedule) at time $t$.

2.  **Forward Simulation (Population Path):**
    *   Initialize the population distribution $x_1(s)$ using the baseline $SS_0$ distribution.
    *   For each year $t = 1 \dots T$:
        *   Aggregate $K_t$ (wealth) and $L_t$ (labor efficiency units) from $x_t(s)$ and policy functions.
        *   Calculate tax revenues ($T_{I,t}, T_{P,t}$) and OASI expenditures.
        *   Update $\psi_t$ to balance the OASI budget.
        *   Update the financing parameter ($tr_{LS, t}$ or $\phi_t$) to balance the general government budget.
        *   Project the distribution to $t+1$: $x_{t+1}(s') = \text{SimulationStep}(x_t, \text{mortality}, \text{policy}_t)$.
        *   Factor in population growth $\nu$ and technological growth $\mu$ to maintain growth-adjusted units.

3.  **Path Update:**
    *   Compute new implied prices $\{r^{implied}_t, w^{implied}_t\}$ from $K_t, L_t$ using the firm's FOCs.
    *   Update the guessed paths using `solver.damping_factor`:
        $$\text{Path}_{new} = (1 - \text{damping}) \cdot \text{Path}_{old} + \text{damping} \cdot \text{Path}_{implied}$$
    *   Update the accidental bequest path $\{q_t\}$ based on the wealth of deceased agents in each year.

### 4. Numerical Considerations

*   **Convergence:** The transition solver requires a significantly lower damping factor (e.g., 0.2) than the steady state to prevent oscillations in the price paths.
*   **State Space Size:** Since policy functions must be stored for every year $t$ and every state $s$, memory management is critical. The forward simulation should be optimized using `numba` to handle the large-scale population update.
*   **Terminal Condition:** Year $T=100$ is assumed to be far enough in the future that the economy has reached the new steady state. The validity of this is checked by ensuring variables are stationary near $t=T$.

### 5. Integration with other Modules

*   **`household_solver.py`:** Must be called in a loop with time-indexed prices and benefit schedules.
*   **`social_security.py`:** Must provide the logic for the linear weighting of the two benefit schedules.
*   **`economy_simulator.py`:** Used in the forward pass to step the distribution $x_t \to x_{t+1}$.
*   **`config.yaml`:** Supplies `mu`, `nu`, `tau_p`, and `transition_years`.

### 6. Logic for Welfare Tracking
During the forward simulation, the module should store the expected lifetime utility for newborn cohorts (age 21) in each year $t$. This data is passed to `evaluation.py` to calculate the Consumption Equivalence Variation (CEV) relative to the baseline. For cohorts alive at $t=1$, the "rest-of-lifetime" utility must be captured to evaluate short-run welfare effects.
