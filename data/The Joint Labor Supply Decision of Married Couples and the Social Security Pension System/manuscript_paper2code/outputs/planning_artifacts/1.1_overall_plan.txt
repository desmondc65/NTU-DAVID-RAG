This plan outlines a step-by-step strategy to reproduce the general-equilibrium overlapping-generations (OLG) model and policy experiments described in the paper.

---

### Phase 1: Model Environment & State Space Discretization
The model relies on a high-dimensional state space. Efficient discretization is critical for computational feasibility.

1.  **State Variables ($s$):**
    *   **Age ($i$):** 21 to 100 (80 periods). Retirement age $I_R = 66$.
    *   **Marital Status ($m$):** 0 (Married), 1 (Single Male), 2 (Single Female).
    *   **Wealth ($a$):** 27 nodes (log-spaced or similar), $A \in [a_{min}, a_{max}]$.
    *   **Historical Earnings ($b_1, b_2$):** 15 nodes each. Used to track AIME for Social Security.
    *   **Productivity Shocks ($e_1, e_2$):** 5 nodes each, discretized using the **Rouwenhorst method** (given the AR(1) specification).

2.  **Parameters & Calibration:**
    *   **Preferences:** $\gamma = 2.0$, $\alpha = 0.653$, joint consumption $\lambda = 0.6$.
    *   **Production:** $\theta = 0.375$ (capital share), $\delta = 0.075$ (depreciation), $\mu = 0.015$ (tech growth), $\nu = 0.009$ (pop growth).
    *   **Survival Rates:** Obtain from SSA 2010 Period Life Table (specified in paper).
    *   **Staying at Home Utility ($\chi$):** Gamma distribution ($\theta_{scale}=2.0$). Parameters $\kappa_1$ and $\kappa_2$ (childcare cost) must be calibrated to match 2013 labor participation.

---

### Phase 2: Solving the Household Problem
The household maximizes lifetime utility through backward induction.

1.  **Terminal Period ($i=100$):**
    *   Value function $v(s, 101) = 0$.
    *   Household consumes all remaining wealth.

2.  **Backward Induction ($i=99 \dots 21$):**
    *   **Leisure/Work Decision:** Households choose $h_1, h_2$ (hours) and $a'$ (next-period wealth).
    *   **Discrete Choice:** Since work participation is discrete (utility from staying at home $\chi$ applies if $h=0$), solve for the value of working ($h>0$) vs. not working ($h=0$).
    *   **Optimization:** Use a Newton-type nonlinear solver (e.g., modified Powell hybrid) to solve Kuhn-Tucker conditions for interior solutions.
    *   **Joint Utility (Married):** Use the unitary model:
        $u_{married} = \tilde{u}_1(c/(1+\lambda), h_1) + \tilde{u}_2(c/(1+\lambda), h_2)$.

3.  **Social Security (OASI) Logic:**
    *   Calculate PIA using the 3-bracket replacement formula (90%, 32%, 15%).
    *   Apply spousal benefits (max of own vs. 50% of spouse) and survivors benefits (max of own vs. 100% of deceased spouse).

---

### Phase 3: Steady-State Baseline Equilibrium
Compute the initial stationary equilibrium (2013 U.S. Economy).

1.  **Algorithm:**
    *   **Outer Loop:** Guess the aggregate capital-labor ratio $(K/L)$ and accidental bequests $q$.
    *   **Inner Loop (Factor Prices):** Calculate $r$ and $w$ from the firm's FOCs.
    *   **Household Step:** Solve the backward induction from Phase 2.
    *   **Simulation Step:** Compute the stationary distribution $x(s)$ by iterating forward from age 21 (starting with $a=0, b=0$).
    *   **Aggregation:** Sum individual wealth and efficiency-weighted labor hours to find $K_{new}$ and $L_{new}$.
    *   **Update:** Adjust guesses for $K, L$, and $q$ (via bisection or Broyden’s method) until markets clear and the government budget balances.

2.  **Government Policy Setup:**
    *   **Income Tax:** Implement Gouveia-Strauss function with parameters $\phi_t, \phi_{m,1}, \phi_{m,2}$.
    *   **OASI:** Fix payroll tax $\tau_P = 0.1007$. Balance OASI budget using the adjustment factor $\psi_t$ (initially 1.0).

---

### Phase 4: Policy Reform & Transition Path
Analyze the cohort-by-cohort removal of spousal and survivors benefits.

1.  **Transition Path Setup:**
    *   Assume the economy reaches a new steady state after $T \approx 100$ years.
    *   **Phasing-in:**
        *   Age $\ge 61$: No change.
        *   Age $\le 21$: Full removal.
        *   Age $22-60$: Linear interpolation of benefit reduction.

2.  **Transition Algorithm:**
    *   Guess the entire path of factor prices $\{r_t, w_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and government variables $\{\psi_t, tr_{LS,t} \text{ or } \phi_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
    *   **Backward Pass:** Solve the household problem along the time path (households now have time-dependent value functions $v_t(s)$).
    *   **Forward Pass:** Start from the baseline distribution $x_0(s)$ and simulate the population forward through the $T$ periods.
    *   **Update Paths:** Check market-clearing and budget-balancing conditions for every year $t$. Update paths until convergence.

---

### Phase 5: Evaluation & Metrics
The results must be compared against the baseline.

1.  **Macroeconomic Metrics:**
    *   $\%$ change in Female Labor Force Participation (LFP).
    *   $\%$ change in Total Output (GDP), Capital Stock ($K$), and Labor Supply ($L$).
    *   $\%$ change in male vs. female work hours.

2.  **Welfare Analysis:**
    *   **Consumption Equivalence Variation ($\lambda_{i,t}$):** Calculate the $\%$ change in baseline consumption required to make a household indifferent to the policy change.
    *   Evaluate for different cohorts (current vs. future) and different wage levels (using the $5 \times 5$ wage grid).

3.  **Sensitivity Analysis (Robustness):**
    *   Run 4: Higher risk aversion ($\gamma=4.0$).
    *   Run 5: Higher wage persistence ($\rho=0.92$).
    *   Run 6: Higher intrafamily wage correlation ($\omega=0.40$).

---

### Summary of Implementation Challenges
*   **Dimensionality:** With 27 assets, 15x15 history, and 5x5 shocks, the state space is large. Vectorized operations and efficient interpolation (e.g., linear or cubic spline) for wealth nodes are necessary.
*   **The $\psi_t$ Adjustment:** Balancing the Social Security budget in every period of the transition is numerically intensive as it requires re-solving the simulation in each iteration.
*   **Integration of $\chi$:** The Gamma distribution for the "stay-at-home" utility must be integrated carefully to compute the expected value $E[v(s')]$ when the participation choice is stochastic at the margin.
