The `data_handler.py` module is responsible for the empirical foundation of the life-cycle model. It processes Taiwan-specific data (HIES, Ministry of Labor, Ministry of Interior) to generate the parameters and profiles required for the simulation.

### 1. Empirical Estimation: Tax Progressivity ($\tau, \lambda$)

The paper uses the Benabou (2002) functional form to approximate Taiwan’s progressive tax system:
$$y^d = \lambda y^{1-\tau}$$
Where $y$ is pre-tax income and $y^d$ is disposable income.

**Logic for `estimate_tax_parameters()`:**
1.  **Data Filtering (HIES):**
    *   Load household-level data from 2014–2021.
    *   **Selection:** Identify "Single-person households" (excluding single parents) and "Married households" (two-person core households).
    *   **Cleaning:** Exclude records with missing tax data or pre-tax income/tax amounts $\le 0$ as per Section 3.4.
    *   **Income Definition:** Pre-tax income ($y$) = reported gross earnings + payroll tax. Disposable income ($y^d$) = pre-tax income - income taxes paid.
2.  **Bracket Partitioning:**
    *   Following the paper's procedure (Section 3.4), partition the sample into income brackets.
    *   For each bracket, calculate the mean pre-tax income and mean disposable income.
3.  **Regression Analysis:**
    *   Perform Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression on the log-transformed equation:
        $$\log(y^d_i) = \log(\lambda) + (1-\tau) \log(y_i) + \epsilon_i$$
    *   **Outputs:**
        *   Slope coefficient ($b$): $\tau = 1 - b$.
        *   Intercept ($a$): $\lambda = \exp(a)$.
4.  **Alignment with Configuration:**
    *   Validate that estimated $\tau_S$ and $\tau_M$ are close to `taxation.progressivity.single` (0.027) and `taxation.progressivity.married` (0.041).
    *   Calibrate $\lambda$ to match the average tax rate observed in the data (10.1% for singles, 14.1% for married) as mentioned in Section 3.4.

### 2. Demographic Profiles: Survival Probabilities ($s_{g,j}$)

The model tracks 15 periods ($J=15$) of 5 years each, starting from age 25.

**Logic for `get_survival_probs()`:**
1.  **Source Data:** 2009–2011 Life Table from the Ministry of Interior.
2.  **Processing:**
    *   Extract annual survival rates for males ($m$) and females ($f$).
    *   **Aggregating to 5-Year Periods:** Since the model period is 5 years, the survival probability $s_{g,j}$ for period $j$ (covering years $t$ to $t+4$) is the probability of surviving the entire 5-year block, conditional on reaching age $t$.
    *   **Working Phase:** For $j < J_r$ (ages 25–64), survival is guaranteed ($s_{g,j} = 1.0$) as per Section 2.1.
    *   **Retirement Phase:** For $j \ge J_r$ (ages 65–99), use gender-specific probabilities.
    *   **Terminal Condition:** $s_{g,15} = 0$ (all households pass away after ages 95–99).
3.  **Output:** A 2D NumPy array of shape `(2, 15)` representing $[gender, period]$.

### 3. Labor Market Profiles: Age-Efficiency ($\epsilon_{g,j}$)

Deterministic human capital accumulation ($\epsilon_{g,j}$) captures gender- and age-specific wage variations.

**Logic for `get_age_efficiency_profiles()`:**
1.  **Source Data:** Ministry of Labor age-wage data (Figure 2 in paper).
2.  **Processing:**
    *   Extract wage profiles for males and females.
    *   **Normalization:** Normalize the profile so the mean efficiency reflects the gender wage gap target in `TABREF1` (0.84).
    *   **Interpolation:** Use cubic spline or linear interpolation to map raw age data into the specific 5-year bins defined in `config.yaml` (`demographics.total_periods`).
3.  **Output:** A 2D NumPy array of shape `(2, 15)`.

### 4. Implementation Details & Data Structures

*   **`load_hies_data(path)`:** 
    *   Must handle multi-year CSV/Stata files. 
    *   Requires mapping HIES classification codes for "Household Organization Type" and "Marital Status" to the model's binary Single/Married categories.
*   **Constants from `config.yaml`:**
    *   `demographics.total_periods` (15) and `demographics.retirement_period` (9) are used to index the arrays.
    *   `taxation.threshold_multiplier` (13.0) is used later in `LifecycleSolver` but the `DataHandler` ensures the OLS regression is performed on data *outside* the top bracket to maintain consistency with Section 3.4.
