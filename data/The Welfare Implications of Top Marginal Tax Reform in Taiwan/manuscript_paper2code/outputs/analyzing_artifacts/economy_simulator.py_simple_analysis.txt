### Logic Analysis: `economy_simulator.py`

The `EconomySimulator` class serves as the orchestration layer for the general equilibrium (GE) solution. It integrates the household-level optimization (from `LifecycleSolver`) with the firm sector and the government's budget constraints to find the steady-state prices and tax rates.

#### 1. General Equilibrium Framework
The model seeks a steady state where:
1.  **Factor Markets Clear**: The capital supplied by households ($K_{supply}$) equals the capital demanded by firms ($K_{demand}$) at the prevailing interest rate $r$.
2.  **Labor Market Consistency**: Aggregate effective labor $N$ is determined by household labor supply decisions given the wage $w$.
3.  **Government Budget Balances**: The insurance premium rate $t$ is adjusted such that total revenue (income tax, consumption tax, and premiums) covers government expenditures ($G$), social security payments ($SS$), and transfers ($T_r$).
4.  **Stationary Distribution**: The distribution of households across states $\Gamma$ is invariant over time.

#### 2. Core Logic and Program Call Flow

##### A. The Steady-State Solver (`solve_steady_state`)
This method implements a nested loop structure to solve for the GE values of $r$ and $t$.

*   **Initialization**:
    *   Convert annual parameters to 5-year equivalents: $\delta = (1 + \delta_{annual})^5 - 1$.
    *   Initial guesses for $r$ (e.g., 0.04 annual $\rightarrow$ 5-year equivalent) and $t$ (insurance premium rate).
*   **Outer Loop (Capital Market Clearing)**:
    *   Update $w$ based on the firm's marginal productivity of labor given current $r$:
        *   From FOC: $w = (1-\alpha) \Psi \left( \frac{\alpha \Psi}{r + \delta} \right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}$.
    *   **Inner Loop (Government Budget Balance)**:
        *   Call `LifecycleSolver.solve_singles` and `LifecycleSolver.solve_married` using current $(r, w, t)$ and the specific `tax_max`.
        *   Call `compute_stationary_distribution` to get $\Gamma$.
        *   Calculate the government budget residual: $Resid_{gov} = Revenue - Expenditure$.
            *   $Exp = G + \text{Pension Payments} + \text{Social Transfers}$.
            *   $Rev = \text{Consumption Taxes} + \text{Income Taxes} + \text{Premium Revenue (from } t)$.
        *   Adjust $t$ (using a root-finder like Brent's method) until $Resid_{gov} < \text{tolerance\_ge}$.
    *   **Market Clearing Check**:
        *   Calculate $K_{supply} = \int k' d\Gamma$.
        *   Calculate $N_{supply} = \int z \epsilon n d\Gamma$.
        *   Calculate $K_{demand}$ using firm FOC: $K_{demand} = N_{supply} \left( \frac{\alpha \Psi}{r + \delta} \right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}$.
        *   Calculate $Resid_{market} = K_{supply} - K_{demand}$.
    *   Adjust $r$ until $Resid_{market} < \text{tolerance\_ge}$.

##### B. Stationary Distribution Calculation (`compute_stationary_distribution`)
This function propagates the mass of households through the state space. Given the complexity (15 age periods, asset grid $k$, productivity states $z$, and historical earnings $\tilde{e}$), this requires efficient iteration.

*   **Mass Initialization**:
    *   Age $j=1$: Set mass based on the population proportions $\phi$ (married) and $(1-\phi)$ (singles).
    *   Assets $k=0$ and average earnings $\tilde{e}=0$.
    *   Productivity $z$: Initial distribution is the stationary distribution of the $4 \times 4$ ordinary Markov matrix (before the superstar state is reachable).
*   **Forward Iteration**:
    *   For each age $j \in [1, J-1]$:
        1.  **State Transitions**: For each state $(k, z, \tilde{e})$, find the optimal next-period asset $k'$ and updated earnings $\tilde{e}'$ from policy functions.
        2.  **Productivity Shift**: Apply the $5 \times 5$ transition matrix $A$ (Table 10) to determine mass movement across $z$.
        3.  **Survival Weighting**: For $j \geq J_r$, multiply the mass by gender-specific survival probabilities $s_{g,j}$.
        4.  **Interpolation**: Since $k'$ and $\tilde{e}'$ might not fall exactly on grid points, use linear interpolation to distribute mass to the nearest neighbors on the grids.
*   **Bequest Collection**:
    *   Sum the assets of households that do not survive to $j+1$.
    *   Distribute these accidental bequests $b$ uniformly to all working-age households in the next iteration.

##### C. Market Clearing and Aggregation (`check_market_clearing`)
This handles the summation of micro-decisions into macro-aggregates:
*   **Aggregate Capital ($K$):** $\sum_{\omega} \Gamma(\omega) k'(\omega)$.
*   **Aggregate Labor ($N$):** $\sum_{\omega} \Gamma(\omega) [z \epsilon_{j,g} n(\omega)]$.
*   **Aggregate Consumption ($C$):** $\sum_{\omega} \Gamma(\omega) c(\omega)$.
*   **Income Tax Revenue:** Apply the Benabou tax function to each household's pre-tax income.
*   **Premium Revenue:** $\sum t \times \text{labor earnings}$.

#### 3. Numerical Considerations (Alignment with Config)
*   **Grids**: Use `config.yaml` values: `k_size` (150) and `e_tilde_size` (40). The asset grid $k$ must be non-linearly spaced (log-scale) to handle the "Superstar" state $z_5$.
*   **Stability**: The "Superstar" state transition (low $\lambda_{in}$, high $\lambda_{stay}$) creates high wealth concentration. The stationary distribution solver must be robust to sparse mass at the top of the grid.
*   **Efficiency**: The distribution iteration is a "non-optimization" loop that can be significantly accelerated with Numba `@jit(nopython=True)`.

#### 4. Alignment with the Paper
*   **Married vs Single**: The simulator must maintain separate distributions for Single Males, Single Females, and Married Households.
*   **Equivalence Scale**: When calculating aggregate consumption tax revenue, ensure married consumption is handled correctly (total consumption $c$ vs. per-capita utility $c/\eta$).
*   **Retirement**: Ensure labor supply $n=0$ for $j \geq J_r$ and that the $\tilde{e}$ state stops updating, correctly triggering the $SS(\tilde{e})$ pension benefit.
