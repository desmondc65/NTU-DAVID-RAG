This plan outlines a detailed roadmap for reproducing the quantitative macroeconomic analysis described in the paper. The focus is on building a heterogeneous-agent, overlapping generations (OLG) life cycle model calibrated to the Taiwanese economy.

---

### 1. Methodology & Model Architecture

The core of the study is a dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) model with idiosyncratic risk.

#### A. Household Structure
*   **Demographics:** $J=15$ periods (each representing 5 years, spanning ages 25 to 99). Retirement age $J_r = 9$ (age 65).
*   **Types:** Single Males ($S, m$), Single Females ($S, f$), and Married Couples ($M$).
*   **Preferences:** Additively separable utility:
    $$u(c, n) = \log c - \theta_g \frac{n^{1+\sigma_g}}{1 + \sigma_g} - \phi_{\iota, g} \mathbb{I}_{n>0}$$
    *   $\sigma_g$: Gender-specific Frisch elasticity.
    *   $\phi_{\iota, g}$: Fixed cost of labor participation (extensive margin).
    *   $\theta_g$: Disutility of work (intensive margin).
*   **Equivalence Scale:** For married households, consumption $c$ is scaled by $\eta = 1.7$ ($u(c/\eta, n_m) + u(c/\eta, n_f)$).

#### B. Income and Productivity
*   **Labor Income:** $w \cdot z \cdot \epsilon_{g,j} \cdot n$.
    *   $z$: Stochastic productivity (5-state Markov process, including a "superstar" state $z_5$).
    *   $\epsilon_{g,j}$: Deterministic age-efficiency profile.
*   **Asset Income:** $r \cdot k$ (No borrowing allowed: $k \geq 0$).
*   **Social Security:** $SS(\tilde{e}) = \psi \tilde{e}$, where $\tilde{e}$ is average lifetime earnings.

#### C. The Tax System (Benabou Functional Form)
The disposable income $y^d$ is modeled as:
$$y^d = \lambda_{\iota} \min\{y_b, y_f\}^{1-\tau_{\iota}} + (1 - \tau_{max}) \max\{0, y_f - y_b\} + T_r - \pi(y_f)$$
*   **Progressivity:** $\tau_{\iota}$ (estimated from HIES data).
*   **Top Marginal Rate:** $\tau_{max}$ (Benchmark: 45%; Reform: 40%).
*   **Threshold $y_b$:** Derived where the log-linear marginal tax equals $\tau_{max}$.

### 2. Implementation Steps

#### Step 1: Data Acquisition & Empirical Estimation
1.  **HIES Data:** Obtain Household Income and Expenditure Survey (2014–2021) from the Survey Research Data Archive (SRDA).
2.  **Estimate Tax Function:** 
    *   Run OLS: $\log(y^d_{i}) = \log \lambda + (1-\tau) \log(y_i) + \epsilon_i$.
    *   Partition sample into Single and Married.
    *   **Target:** $\tau_S \approx 0.027$ and $\tau_M \approx 0.041$.
3.  **Survival Probabilities:** Incorporate the 2009–2011 Life Table from the Ministry of Interior.
4.  **Labor Profiles:** Use Ministry of Labor data for gender-specific age-efficiency $\epsilon_{g,j}$.

#### Step 2: Parameter Calibration
*   **Preset Parameters:**
    *   $\alpha$ (Capital share) = 0.34.
    *   $\delta$ (Depreciation) = 9.05% (annual).
    *   $\tau_c$ (Consumption tax) = 5%.
    *   $J, J_r$: 15 and 9.
*   **Calibrated Parameters (Moment Matching):**
    *   $\beta$ (Discount factor): Match $K/Y = 2.1$.
    *   $\Psi$ (TFP): Set to 1.78 to normalize $w = 1$.
    *   $\phi_{\iota, g}$ & $\theta_g$: Match labor participation and average hours (Table 10).
    *   $z_5, \lambda_{in}, \lambda_{stay}$: Jointly calibrate to match the top 1% and 10% income shares and Gini (0.60).

#### Step 3: Solving the Household Problem
Use **Value Function Iteration (VFI)** or the **Endogenous Grid Method (EGM)**.
*   **State Space:** $\{j, k, z, \tilde{e}\}$ for singles; $\{j, k, z_m, z_f, \tilde{e}\}$ for married.
*   **Married Complexity:** Note that married households solve for two labor supplies ($n_m, n_f$).
*   **Average Earnings:** Discretize $\tilde{e}$ to track pension benefits accurately.

#### Step 4: Finding the Steady-State Equilibrium
1.  **Outer Loop:** Guess interest rate $r$ (and thus $K/N$).
2.  **Inner Loop:** Guess insurance premium rate $t$.
3.  **Simulation:** 
    *   Compute policy functions for $c, k', n$.
    *   Compute stationary distribution $\Gamma$ using the transition matrix and policy functions.
4.  **Market Clearing:**
    *   Check if $K_{supply} = K_{demand}$.
    *   Check if Government Budget Balances: $G + SS\_Benefits = Tax\_Rev + \pi\_Rev$.
5.  **Update:** Adjust $r$ and $t$ using a quasi-Newton method until convergence.

### 3. Experimental Settings & Evaluation

#### A. Policy Scenarios
1.  **Benchmark:** $\tau_{max} = 0.45, y_b \approx 13 \times$ average income.
2.  **Tax Reform:** Change $\tau_{max}$ to 0.40. Keep $G$ constant. Adjust $t$ to balance the budget.
3.  **Optimization:** Iterate $\tau_{max}$ from 0.0 to 0.9 to find the welfare-maximizing peak.

#### B. Evaluation Metrics
*   **Aggregates:** Change in GDP ($Y$), Capital ($K$), and Market Wage ($w$).
*   **Inequality:** Gini coefficients (Wealth, Income, Consumption) and Top 1% shares.
*   **Welfare ($W_N, W_U$):** 
    *   **Newborn ($W_N$):** Consumption equivalent variation for a household entering the economy "under the veil of ignorance."
    *   **Utilitarian ($W_U$):** Aggregated lifetime utility of all currently living households.

### 4. Technical Requirements & Potential Challenges

*   **Software:** Python (Scientific stack: `NumPy`, `SciPy`, `Numba` for JIT compilation of VFI loops).
*   **Computational Intensity:** Solving the married household's Bellman equation is computationally expensive due to the dimensionality of productivity ($z_m \times z_f$) and the joint labor decision. 
    *   *Optimization:* Use parallel processing for the age-specific value function steps.
*   **Grid Choice:** Use a non-linear (log-spaced) grid for assets $k$ to capture the behavior of both the poor (liquidity constraints) and the "superstar" wealthy.
*   **Unclear Aspect:** The paper doesn't explicitly state the size of the grids for $k$ and $\tilde{e}$. A standard starting point would be $k \in [0, 1000]$ with 100–200 points and $\tilde{e}$ with 20–50 points.
