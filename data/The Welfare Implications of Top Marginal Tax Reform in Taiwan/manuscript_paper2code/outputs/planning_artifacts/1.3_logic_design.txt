[Logic Analysis]
- ['parameters.py', 'Contains the ModelParams class. It defines all exogenous parameters (alpha=0.34, delta=0.0905 annual, tau_c=0.05), calibration targets (K/Y=2.1, Top 1% share), and grid specifications. It sets up the 5x5 productivity transition matrix (Table 10) and discretized grids for assets (k) using a log-scale to capture the top 1% tail and historical earnings (e_tilde) for pension calculations.']
- ['data_handler.py', 'Implements the DataHandler class. It processes the HIES microdata to: 1) Estimate Benabou tax progressivity (tau_s, tau_m) using OLS regression on log(pre-tax) and log(after-tax) income. 2) Interpolate age-efficiency profiles (epsilon) from Ministry of Labor data. 3) Extract survival probabilities (s_g,j) from Ministry of Interior life tables. It outputs formatted arrays used by the LifecycleSolver.']
- ['lifecycle_solver.py', 'Implements the LifecycleSolver class. This is the computational core. It uses Numba-accelerated Value Function Iteration (VFI) to solve the Bellman equations for Single (Male/Female) and Married households. For Married households, it solves the joint labor supply problem (n_m, n_f) and consumption sharing (equivalence scale eta=1.7). It handles the transition between working life (ages 25-64) and retirement (ages 65-99) where labor income is replaced by pensions (SS).']
- ['economy_simulator.py', "Implements the EconomySimulator class. It performs two main functions: 1) Computation of the stationary distribution (Gamma) by iterating the distribution through policy functions and the Markov transition matrix. 2) The General Equilibrium (GE) loop. It uses a root-finding algorithm (e.g., Brent's method) to find the interest rate (r) that clears capital markets and a secondary loop to find the insurance premium rate (t) that balances the government budget given tax revenues and pension expenditures."]
- ['welfare_analyst.py', 'Contains the WelfareAnalyst class. It calculates the Newborn Welfare (W_N) and Utilitarian Welfare (W_U) using the policy functions and stationary distributions. It implements the Consumption Equivalent Variation (CEV) formula to determine the uniform percentage change in consumption required to make agents indifferent between the benchmark and a tax reform scenario.']
- ['visualizer.py', 'Handles data visualization. Functions to generate: 1) Life-cycle consumption/labor profiles (Figure 5). 2) Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients for income/wealth/consumption (Figure 8). 3) Welfare curves across a range of top marginal tax rates (Figure 4) to identify the 69% peak.']
- ['main.py', 'The entry point script. It orchestrates the workflow: 1) Trigger empirical data estimation. 2) Solve the benchmark steady state (tau_max=0.45). 3) Run the 2017 reform scenario (tau_max=0.40). 4) Run a grid search over tau_max [0.0, 0.9] to find the optimal 69% rate. 5) Output the comparison tables (Tables 5 and 6).']

[Shared Knowledge]
1. The 'Model Period' is 5 years; all annual rates (delta, r, beta) must be converted to 5-year equivalents: (1+r_annual)^5 - 1. 2. The 'Asset Grid' must be dense at the bottom to handle borrowing constraints (k>=0) and sparse but very long at the top to capture 'Superstar' wealth. 3. 'Numba JIT' is used across LifecycleSolver and EconomySimulator to ensure the GE loops converge in reasonable time (hours rather than days). 4. 'Global State' such as wage (w) is normalized to 1.0 via the TFP parameter (Psi) during calibration.
