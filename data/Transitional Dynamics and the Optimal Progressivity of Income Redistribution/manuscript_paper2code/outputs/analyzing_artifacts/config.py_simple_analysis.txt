This analysis focuses on the `config.py` file, which serves as the central repository for all calibrated parameters, grid definitions, and model-period adjustments. The implementation will strictly adhere to the values defined in `config.yaml` and the mathematical transformations required to convert annual data into the 5-year model period used in the paper.

### 1. Parameter Mapping and Period Adjustments
The paper specifies that the model period is **5 years**. Consequently, annual rates provided in the `config.yaml` must be compounded or adjusted to reflect this duration.

*   **Depreciation ($\delta$):** The annual depreciation rate is 0.08. For a 5-year period:
    $$\delta_{period} = 1 - (1 - \delta_{annual})^5 \approx 1 - (1 - 0.08)^5 \approx 0.3409$$
    *Referencing `economy.annual_depreciation` and `economy.model_period_years`.*
*   **Discount Factor ($\beta$):** The annual discount factor is 0.962. For a 5-year period:
    $$\beta_{period} = (\beta_{annual})^5 \approx (0.962)^5 \approx 0.8225$$
    *Referencing `household.annual_discount_factor` and `economy.model_period_years`.*
*   **Target Interest Rate ($r$):** The target annual interest rate is 4.1%. For a 5-year period:
    $$r_{target, period} = (1 + r_{annual})^5 - 1 \approx (1.041)^5 - 1 \approx 0.2224$$
    *Referencing `economy.target_annual_interest_rate`.*

### 2. Physical Constants and Preferences
These values are mapped directly from the `config.yaml` without transformation, aligning with the "Data structures and interfaces" design:

*   **`sigma` (Risk Aversion):** Mapped from `household.risk_aversion` (2.0).
*   **`alpha` (Capital Share):** Mapped from `economy.capital_share` (0.36).
*   **`mu` (Death Probability):** Mapped from `productivity.prob_death` (0.2). This represents a 20% chance of dying every 5 years, consistent with a 25-year generation length.
*   **`theta` (Labor Scale):** Mapped from `household.labor_disutility_scale` (0.358).
*   **`epsilon` (Labor Curvature):** Mapped from `household.labor_supply_curvature` (1.183).
*   **`tau_status_quo`:** Mapped from `tax_policy.benchmark_tau` (0.17).
*   **`g_y_ratio`:** Mapped from `economy.government_exp_ratio` (0.17).

### 3. Grid Generation Logic
The state space consists of assets ($k$) and productivity ($z$).

#### 3.1 Asset Grid (`k_grid`)
To accurately capture the non-linear behavior near the borrowing constraint ($k=0$), a **log-spaced grid** is required.
*   **Range:** $[k_{min}, k_{max}]$ from `numerical.k_min` (0.0) and `numerical.k_max` (3000.0).
*   **Density:** `numerical.k_size` (200 points).
*   **Logic:**
    1.  Create a linear space in logs: `temp_grid = np.geomspace(1.0, k_max + 1.0, k_size)`.
    2.  Shift back to start at zero: `k_grid = temp_grid - 1.0`.
    This ensures more grid points are allocated to lower wealth levels where the consumption-savings curvature is highest.

#### 3.2 Productivity Grid (`z_grid`)
The labor efficiency units $z$ are composed of an intergenerational component $f$ and a life-cycle component $a$. The paper identifies 4 discrete states based on the combinations of $f \in \{f_L, f_H\}$ and $a \in \{a_L, a_H\}$.
*   **States:**
    *   $z_1 = f_L + a_L$
    *   $z_2 = f_L + a_H$
    *   $z_3 = f_H + a_L$
    *   $z_4 = f_H + a_H$
*   **Values:** From `productivity.f_states` ([-0.40, 0.55]) and `productivity.a_states` ([-0.37, 0.33]). Note: These are logs or deviations; the actual efficiency units used in $y = zwh + rk$ must be handled as $z = \exp(\text{component sums})$ or as direct additives depending on the model's wage normalization. Based on Section 3.2, these are standard deviations/states for logs of efficiency. However, the algorithm specifies "states imply four possible values for hourly wages", which we will treat as the direct additive efficiency units $z$ in the code to match the $y = zwh + rk$ specification.

### 4. Convergence and Numerical Settings
The following parameters from `numerical` in `config.yaml` will be stored to control the solvers:
*   `tol_ss` (1e-6): Tolerance for steady-state price and distribution convergence.
*   `tol_path` (1e-4): Tolerance for the transition path's price vector convergence.
*   `damping` (0.2): Weight for the convex combination when updating guesses for $r$ and $\lambda$.
*   `transition_horizon` (30): Total periods $T$ for the transition simulation.

### 5. Final Data Structure Interface
The `GlobalConfig` class will be implemented as a `dataclass` to ensure immutability and easy access across the `model` and `solvers` packages. It will encapsulate:
1.  **Direct parameters** (alpha, sigma, mu, etc.).
2.  **Adjusted parameters** (delta, beta).
3.  **Generated arrays** (k_grid, z_grid).
4.  **Targets** (target_r, g_y_ratio).

This structure ensures that any change in `config.yaml` automatically propagates correctly through the necessary period-conversions before reaching the physics engine.
