The logic analysis for `main.py` describes the high-level orchestration of the experiments required to reproduce the paper's findings. This module acts as the central coordinator, utilizing the `EconomyConfig`, `SteadyStateSolver`, `TransitionSolver`, and `WelfareAnalysis` classes as defined in the architectural design.

### 1. Initialization and Configuration
*   **Load Parameters:** Load global constants from `config.yaml` (e.g., $\sigma=2.0$, $\alpha=0.36$, $\mu=0.2$, $G/Y=0.17$).
*   **Grid Setup:** Initialize `EconomyConfig`. This involves constructing the non-linear capital grid $k$ using `numerical.k_grid_exp` to ensure higher density near the borrowing constraint ($k=0$) and setting up the 4-state labor productivity vector $z$ (combinations of $f_L, f_H$ and $a_L, a_H$).
*   **Matrix Construction:** Invoke `Calibration` logic to build the aggregate transition matrix $P = \mu D + (1-\mu)S$, where $D$ and $S$ are generated from the PSID-based estimates provided in Section 3.2 of the paper.

### 2. Experiment 1: The U.S. Status Quo (Benchmark)
*   **Objective:** Solve for the general equilibrium where $\tau = 0.17$.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Initialize `SteadyStateSolver`.
    2.  Call `find_equilibrium(tau=0.17)`.
    3.  **Solver Logic:** The solver must perform a nested iteration:
        *   *Outer loop:* Guess $\lambda$ to balance the budget $g = 0.17 \times Y$.
        *   *Inner loop:* Guess $r$ to clear the capital market.
        *   *Policy solver:* Invoke `HouseholdSolver` to get $k'(k,z)$ and $h(k,z)$ via EGM and labor FOC bisection.
        *   *Distribution:* Compute stationary $\Gamma(k,z)$.
    4.  **Store Baseline:** Capture $K, N, Y, C, w, r, \lambda$, and Gini coefficients (Wealth: 0.53, Consumption: 0.17, etc.) for Table 2.

### 3. Experiment 2: Steady-State Optimal Tax Search
*   **Objective:** Identify $\tau_{SS}^*$ that maximizes $W_{SS} = \int V_{SS}(k, z; \lambda, \tau) d\Gamma_{SS}$.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Perform a grid search over $\tau \in [-0.2, 0.4]$.
    2.  For each $\tau$, solve the steady-state equilibrium.
    3.  Evaluate the utilitarian social welfare function at the stationary distribution.
    4.  **Comparison:** Compare the "Optimal" SS ($\tau = -0.09$) with the "U.S." SS.
    5.  **Output Table 2:** Generate the comparative table showing % changes in Output (+18.4%) and Consumption (+16.1%).

### 4. Experiment 3: Optimal Reform with Transitional Dynamics
*   **Objective:** Solve the "Once-and-for-all" reform problem, taking into account the path from $\Gamma_{SQ}$ to $\Gamma_{new}$.
*   **Transition Scenarios:**
    1.  **Case $\beta_g = 0$:** Solve for the $\tau$ that maximizes welfare for the current generation only.
    2.  **Case $\beta_g = \beta$:** Solve for the $\tau$ when the policymaker values future generations like altruistic parents.
    3.  **Case $\beta_g \to 1$:** Observe the convergence of optimal $\tau$ toward the steady-state optimal value (-0.09).
*   **Transition Logic:**
    1.  Initialize `TransitionSolver` with the start ($\tau=0.17$) and target ($\tau_{new}$) steady states.
    2.  Invoke `solve_path(t_periods=50)`.
    3.  **Backwards/Forwards Loop:**
        *   Guess paths $\{r_t, \lambda_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
        *   Solve household policies backwards from $V_{SS, new}$.
        *   Simulate distribution $\Gamma_t$ forwards from $\Gamma_{SQ}$.
        *   Update paths using damping factors (`damping_r`, `damping_lambda`) until the market clears and the budget balances in every period.

### 5. Experiment 4: Efficiency vs. Equity (Certainty Equivalent)
*   **Objective:** Isolate the insurance motive from the pure redistribution motive using the "Efficiency Criterion" (Eq. 7).
*   **Process:**
    1.  Use the `WelfareAnalysis` class to decompose the value function into consumption and labor components.
    2.  Calculate the certainty equivalent consumption $\tilde{c}$ and hours $\tilde{n}$ for the whole transition path.
    3.  Determine the optimal $\tau$ under this criterion (Paper result: $\tau = 0.10$ for $\beta_g = \beta$ scenario).

### 6. Data Synthesis and Visualization
*   **Table 3 Generation:** Compute the distribution of the tax burden across income deciles for the benchmark and optimal transition scenarios.
*   **Figure 1:** Plot the tax function fit against pre-tax income.
*   **Figure 2:** Plot Welfare vs. Wealth for different productivity levels.
*   **Figure 3:** Plot the time series of $K_t, N_t, C_t$, and Gini coefficients along the transition path to regressive taxation.
*   **Figure 4:** Plot the relationship between the policymaker's discount factor $\beta_g$ and the resulting optimal $\tau$.

### 7. Constraints and Convergence check
*   Ensure that for $\tau < 0$, the budget balance loop in `SteadyStateSolver` handles the potential for high-income transfers correctly.
*   Verify that the transition length $T=50$ (250 years) is sufficient for the capital stock $K$ to reach the new steady-state neighborhood.
*   Monitor convergence of $r$ and $\lambda$ using tolerances `tol_ge` and `tol_ss` from `config.yaml`.
