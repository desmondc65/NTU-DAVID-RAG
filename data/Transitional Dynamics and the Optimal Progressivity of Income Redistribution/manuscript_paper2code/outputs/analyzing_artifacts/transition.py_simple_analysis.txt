### Logic Analysis: `transition.py`

The `TransitionSolver` class is responsible for computing the non-linear path of the economy from the U.S. status quo steady state (SS1) to a new steady state (SS2) defined by a target progressivity parameter $\tau_{new}$. This process requires solving for a sequence of prices and tax scaling factors that satisfy general equilibrium and budget balance at every point in time.

#### 1. Integration with Design and Configuration
*   **Constants from `config.yaml`**: 
    *   `numerical.transition_periods` ($T=50$) defines the time horizon.
    *   `numerical.damping_r` and `numerical.damping_lambda` control the update speed of the paths.
    *   `numerical.tol_ge` and `numerical.max_iter` guide the convergence of the path sequence.
    *   `economy.g_y_ratio`: The level of government expenditure $g$ is fixed at $0.17 \times Y_{benchmark}$ (from the status quo SS).
*   **Interface Dependency**: 
    *   Uses `HouseholdSolver.solve_backward_step` to find time-varying policies.
    *   Inputs `ss_start` and `ss_end` from `SteadyStateSolver` to provide initial distribution $\Gamma_0$ and terminal value function $V_T$.

#### 2. Core Logic Flow: `solve_path(t_periods, beta_g)`

The solver implements a fixed-point iteration over the path of aggregate variables.

**A. Initialization**
*   **State Preparation**: Extract the stationary distribution $\Gamma_0$ from `ss_start` and the terminal value function $V_T$ from `ss_end`.
*   **Path Guessing**: Initialize vectors $\{r_t\}_{t=1}^T$ and $\{\lambda_t\}_{t=1}^T$. A robust starting guess is the values from the terminal steady state (SS2).
*   **Wage Sequence**: Calculate the corresponding wage sequence $\{w_t\}_{t=1}^T$ based on the guess $\{r_t\}$.

**B. Outer Loop: Path Convergence (Iterate until $\{r_t, \lambda_t\}$ converge)**
1.  **Backward Induction (Household Problem)**:
    *   Set $V_{T+1} = V_{SS2}$.
    *   For $t = T$ down to $1$:
        *   Call `hh_solver.solve_backward_step(r_t, w_t, lambda_t, V_{t+1})`.
        *   This step uses the Endogenous Gridpoint Method (EGM) to find policy functions $k'_t(k, z)$ and $h_t(k, z)$, and calculates the current value function $V_t(k, z)$ to be used by period $t-1$.
    *   Store the sequence of `HouseholdPolicy` objects.

2.  **Forward Simulation (Aggregation and Distribution)**:
    *   Start with the distribution $\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_0$ (Wealth is predetermined).
    *   For $t = 1$ to $T$:
        *   **Aggregates**: Calculate $K_t = \int k \, d\Gamma_t$ and $N_t = \int z h_t(k, z) \, d\Gamma_t$.
        *   **Implied Prices**: 
            *   $r_{implied, t} = \alpha (K_t/N_t)^{\alpha-1} - \delta$
            *   $w_{implied, t} = (1-\alpha) (K_t/N_t)^{-\alpha}$
        *   **Budget Balance**: Calculate total tax revenue using policies $\{k'_t, h_t\}$ and $\Gamma_t$. Find $\lambda_{implied, t}$ such that government revenue equals the fixed $g$.
        *   **Update Distribution**: Compute $\Gamma_{t+1}$ using the savings policy $k'_t(k, z)$ and the aggregate transition matrix $P = (1-\mu)S + \mu D$. 
            *   *Note*: The transition logic must account for the fact that a fraction $\mu$ are replaced by descendants whose productivity is drawn from the $D$ matrix.

3.  **Path Update**:
    *   Compare $\{r_{implied, t}\}$ and $\{\lambda_{implied, t}\}$ against the current guesses.
    *   Update guesses using convex combinations (damping):
        *   $r_{new, t} = (1 - \text{damping}_r) r_{guess, t} + \text{damping}_r r_{implied, t}$
        *   $\lambda_{new, t} = (1 - \text{damping}_\lambda) \lambda_{guess, t} + \text{damping}_\lambda \lambda_{implied, t}$

**C. Welfare Analysis**
*   Once paths converge, calculate the Social Welfare Function (SWF):
    $$W = \int V_1(k, z) \, d\Gamma_0 + \mu \sum_{t=1}^T \beta_g^t \int V_{t+1}(k, z) \, d\Gamma_{newborn, t+1}$$
*   This incorporates the welfare of those alive at the time of reform ($t=1$) and the discounted welfare of all future generations.

#### 3. Numerical Considerations
*   **Boundary Conditions**: While $T=50$ is usually sufficient for convergence to the neighborhood of the steady state, the solver should verify that $r_T \approx r_{SS2}$ and $\lambda_T \approx \lambda_{SS2}$.
*   **Efficiency**: The forward simulation of the distribution $\Gamma_t$ and the backward induction should be JIT-compiled (using `@njit`) to handle the $k\_grid \times z\_states$ dimensions efficiently over $T$ periods.
*   **Interpolation**: During the forward simulation, the savings policy $k'_t(k, z)$ will likely map to points off the $k\_grid$; linear interpolation is required to distribute the mass into the bins of $\Gamma_{t+1}$.
