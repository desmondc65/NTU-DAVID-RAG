This logic analysis for `utils.py` focuses on the mathematical implementation of the fiscal system, preferences, and the intratemporal labor supply optimization required for the dynastic Aiyagari-Bewley-Huggett model.

### 1. Fiscal System Logic
The tax system is defined by the non-linear relationship between gross income ($y$) and disposable income ($y^d$).

*   **Gross Income ($y$):** 
    $$y = zwh + rk$$
    Where $z$ is labor efficiency, $w$ is the efficiency wage, $h$ is hours worked, $r$ is the net interest rate, and $k$ is current capital.
*   **Disposable Income Function:** 
    Following Equation (2) in the paper: 
    $$y^d(y) = \lambda y^{1-\tau}$$
    *   **$\tau$ (Progressivity):** From `config.taxation.tau_status_quo` (0.17) or experimental values. $\tau > 0$ is progressive, $\tau = 0$ is proportional, $\tau < 0$ is regressive.
    *   **$\lambda$ (Scaling):** Determined endogenously to satisfy the government budget $G = 0.17 \times Y$.
*   **Marginal Net Income ($\frac{\partial y^d}{\partial y}$):**
    This is critical for the household's first-order conditions:
    $$\frac{\partial y^d}{\partial y} = \lambda (1-\tau) y^{-\tau}$$
*   **Marginal Tax Rate (MTR):**
    $$MTR(y) = 1 - \frac{\partial y^d}{\partial y} = 1 - \lambda (1-\tau) y^{-\tau}$$

### 2. Preference and Utility Logic
The model uses a separable CRRA utility function with respect to consumption and a power disutility for labor.

*   **Utility Function:**
    $$u(c, h) = \frac{c^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma} - \theta \frac{h^{1+\epsilon}}{1+\epsilon}$$
    *   $\sigma$: `economy.sigma` (2.0).
    *   $\theta$: `preferences.theta` (0.358).
    *   $\epsilon$: `preferences.epsilon` (1.183).
*   **Marginal Utility of Consumption ($u_c$):**
    $$u_c = c^{-\sigma}$$
*   **Marginal Disutility of Labor ($u_h$):**
    $$u_h = -\theta h^\epsilon$$

### 3. Intratemporal Labor Supply Logic (Bisection)
Given a specific capital choice $k'$ (from the EGM step in `household.py`) and current assets $k$, the agent chooses hours $h \in (0, 1)$ to satisfy the intratemporal FOC.

*   **The FOC Equation:**
    The marginal disutility of labor must equal the marginal utility of consumption multiplied by the marginal after-tax labor income:
    $$\theta h^\epsilon = c^{-\sigma} \cdot \left[ \frac{\partial y^d}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial h} \right]$$
    Substituting the derivatives:
    $$\theta h^\epsilon = c^{-\sigma} \cdot \left[ \lambda (1-\tau) (zwh + rk)^{-\tau} \cdot zw \right]$$
    Where consumption $c$ is substituted from the budget constraint:
    $$c = \lambda (zwh + rk)^{1-\tau} + k - k'$$

*   **Bisection Function ($f(h)$):**
    We find the root $h \in [1e-6, 1-1e-6]$ for:
    $$f(h) = \theta h^\epsilon - (\lambda (zwh + rk)^{1-\tau} + k - k')^{-\sigma} \cdot \lambda (1-\tau) (zwh + rk)^{-\tau} zw$$

*   **Numerical Implementation Details:**
    1.  **Lower Bound ($h_{min}$):** Near 0 (e.g., $10^{-7}$).
    2.  **Upper Bound ($h_{max}$):** Near 1 (e.g., $1-10^{-7}$).
    3.  **Consumption check:** If $c \le 0$ at $h_{min}$, the labor supply is invalid for the chosen $k'$, suggesting $k'$ is too high (this is handled by the EGM logic).
    4.  **Convergence:** Iterate until $|f(h)| < 10^{-8}$ or the interval is sufficiently small.
    5.  **Performance:** Since this bisection is nested within the household solver's asset grid and productivity states, it should be optimized using `numba.njit`.

### 4. Certainty Equivalent Logic (Efficiency Criterion)
To support the "Efficiency vs. Equity" experiment (Section 2.3), utils must provide components to compute Equation (7):
*   **Consumption Certainty Equivalent ($\tilde{c}$):** Solve $V_c = \frac{1}{1-\beta} \frac{\tilde{c}^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}$ for $\tilde{c}$.
*   **Leisure Certainty Equivalent ($\tilde{h}$):** Solve $V_l = \frac{1}{1-\beta} \theta \frac{\tilde{h}^{1+\epsilon}}{1+\epsilon}$ for $\tilde{h}$.
*   **Welfare Aggregation:** $\int (\tilde{c}(k,z) - \tilde{n}(k,z)) d\Gamma(k,z)$ (Note: $\tilde{n}$ here is the work component).

### 5. Summary of Data and Configuration Usage
*   **`economy` params:** Uses `sigma`, `alpha`, `delta_annual`, `period_years`.
*   **`preferences` params:** Uses `theta`, `epsilon`.
*   **`taxation` params:** Uses `tau_status_quo`.
*   **Numerical stability:** The bisection must handle $y=0$ cases (especially when $\tau > 0$, where $y^{-\tau}$ is undefined). A small epsilon should be added to $y$ to prevent division by zero.

This logic ensures that the household's labor response to the non-linear tax code—a central mechanism in the paper—is captured accurately.
