This plan outlines a comprehensive strategy to reproduce the findings of the paper "Review of Economic Dynamics" regarding optimal non-linear tax policy. Since the authors did not release code, we will implement a dynastic Aiyagari-style model with endogenous labor supply and transitional dynamics.

---

### Phase 1: Model Environment and Calibration
The first step is to define the economic parameters and the stochastic processes that drive the model.

#### 1.1 Preferences and Production
*   **Utility Function:** $u(c, h) = \frac{c^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma} - \theta \frac{h^{1+\epsilon}}{1+\epsilon}$.
*   **Production Function:** $Y = K^\alpha N^{1-\alpha}$.
*   **Parameters (Table 1):**
    *   Risk aversion $\sigma = 2.0$.
    *   Capital share $\alpha = 0.36$.
    *   Annual depreciation $\delta_{annual} = 0.08$. (Model period is 5 years: $\delta = 1 - (1-0.08)^5 \approx 0.34$).
    *   Discount factor $\beta$: Calibrated to match $r=4.1\%$ (approx. $0.962^5$ per period).
    *   Labor parameters $\theta, \epsilon$: Calibrated to $h=0.49$ and $CV(h)=0.27$.

#### 1.2 The Productivity Process ($z$)
The skill level $z$ is the sum of an intergenerational component $f$ and a life-cycle component $a$.
*   **States:** 
    *   $f \in \{-0.40, +0.55\}$
    *   $a \in \{-0.37, +0.33\}$
*   **Transition Matrices:**
    *   Survival (Matrix $S$): $f$ stays constant; $a$ follows matrix $A$.
    *   Death/Rebirth (Matrix $D$): New $f'$ follows matrix $F$; new $a'$ starts at $a_L$ with probability $\pi = 0.9$.
*   **Combined Matrix:** $P = \mu D + (1-\mu)S$, where $\mu = 0.2$ (25-year generation).

#### 1.3 The Tax Function
*   **Form:** $y_d = \lambda (zwh + rk)^{1-\tau}$.
*   **Status Quo:** $\tau = 0.17$.
*   **Budget Balance:** $\lambda$ must be solved endogenously such that $\int [y - y_d(y)] d\Gamma = g$, where $g = 0.17 \times Y_{benchmark}$.

---

### Phase 2: Steady-State Solver
We need a robust algorithm to find the stationary equilibrium for any given $\tau$.

#### 2.1 State Space and Grids
*   **Assets ($k$):** Create a non-linear grid (denser near the borrowing constraint $k=0$).
*   **Productivity ($z$):** 4 states ($f_L+a_L, f_L+a_H, f_H+a_L, f_H+a_H$).

#### 2.2 Household Optimization (Inner Loop)
*   **Algorithm:** Endogenous Grid Method (EGM) for the saving policy $k'(k, z)$.
*   **Labor Supply:** For each $k'$, solve the FOC for $h$ using a bisection method:
    $(1-\tau) \lambda (zwh + rk)^{-\tau} zw [c(h)]^{-\sigma} = \theta h^\epsilon$.
*   **Iteration:** Iterate on the value function or policy function until $k'(k, z)$ converges.

#### 2.3 General Equilibrium (Outer Loop)
1.  **Guess** $r$ and $\lambda$.
2.  **Compute** $w = (1-\alpha)(\frac{\alpha}{r+\delta})^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}$.
3.  **Solve** Household problem.
4.  **Compute Stationary Distribution:** Iterate $\Gamma_{n+1} = H(\Gamma_n)$ until $\Gamma$ is stable.
5.  **Check Market Clearing:**
    *   Implied $K = \int k d\Gamma$.
    *   Implied $N = \int zh d\Gamma$.
    *   New $r_{calc} = \alpha (K/N)^{\alpha-1} - \delta$.
    *   New $\lambda_{calc}$ from budget balance.
6.  **Update** $(r, \lambda)$ using a convex combination (damping) until convergence.

---

### Phase 3: Transitional Dynamics Solver
To find the optimal reform, we must simulate the path from the U.S. Steady State ($\tau=0.17$) to a new Steady State ($\tau_{new}$).

#### 3.1 Setup
*   **Horizon:** Set $T \approx 30$ periods (150 years) to ensure convergence.
*   **Boundary Conditions:** Initial distribution $\Gamma_0$ is the U.S. steady state. Terminal value function $V_T$ is the new steady state value function.

#### 3.2 Path Iteration
1.  **Guess** paths $\{r_t, \lambda_t\}_{t=1}^T$.
2.  **Backward Induction:** Starting from $V_T$, solve the household's problem for $t = T-1, T-2, \dots, 0$ to get a sequence of policy functions $\{k'_t, h_t\}$.
3.  **Forward Simulation:** Starting from $\Gamma_0$, apply the sequence of policy functions to compute $\{\Gamma_t, K_t, N_t\}$ for $t=1, \dots, T$.
4.  **Update Paths:**
    *   Calculate $r_{t, calc}$ from $K_t$ and $N_t$.
    *   Calculate $\lambda_{t, calc}$ from the budget constraint at each $t$.
5.  **Convergence:** Check if the guessed paths match the calculated paths. If not, update guesses and repeat.

---

### Phase 4: Optimal Policy Search
We will evaluate welfare for different values of $\tau$ (e.g., from $-0.2$ to $0.5$).

#### 4.1 Objective Functions
1.  **Steady-State Utilitarian:** $W_{SS} = \int V_{SS}(k, z; \tau) d\Gamma_{SS}$.
2.  **Transition Utilitarian:** 
    $W_{trans} = \int V_0(k, z; \tau) d\Gamma_0 + \mu \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \beta_g^t \int V_t(k, z; \tau) d\Gamma_t$.
    *   *Note:* The term $\beta_g$ represents how much the policymaker weights future generations.
3.  **Efficiency Criterion:** Compute Certainty Equivalents for consumption and leisure separately (as per Section 2.3).

---

### Phase 5: Evaluation and Metrics
To match the paper's results, we will track:
*   **Aggregates:** Output ($Y$), Capital ($K$), Hours ($N$), and Consumption ($C$).
*   **Inequality:** Gini coefficients for Wealth, Pre-tax Income, Disposable Income, and Consumption.
*   **Welfare Gain:** Calculate the "Consumption Equivalent Variation"—the % increase in benchmark consumption needed to make agents indifferent to the reform.

---

### Implementation Notes & Potential Challenges
*   **Computational Efficiency:** The transition path is computationally expensive. Parallelizing the policy function solvers for each productivity state will be necessary.
*   **Grid Sensitivity:** The paper notes that the bottom 10% of households hold no wealth. The asset grid must be very fine near zero to capture the behavior of liquidity-constrained agents.
*   **$\lambda$ Adjustment:** In every period of the transition, the government budget must balance exactly. This makes the path of $\lambda_t$ highly sensitive to shocks in aggregate income.
*   **Unclear Aspect:** The exact number of grid points for $k$ isn't specified; standard practice is 100–500 points with a log-scale.
*   **Certainty Equivalence:** The decomposition of utility into consumption and labor certainty equivalents requires careful bookkeeping of the discounted sums $V_c$ and $V_l$ during the EGM and backward induction steps.
