[Logic Analysis]
- ['config.py', "Contains a `GlobalConfig` dataclass storing all calibrated parameters from Table 1: sigma (2.0), alpha (0.36), annual delta (0.08), mu (0.2), annual interest rate target (4.1%), etc. It also defines grid parameters like 'k_grid' (min=0, max=5000, 200 points log-spaced) and 'tau' ranges."]
- ['model/productivity.py', "Implements the `ProductivityProcess` class. It constructs the block-diagonal Matrix S (survival) and Matrix D (death/rebirth) using the paper's estimates for F and A. Methods: `get_combined_transition_matrix()` calculates (mu*D + (1-mu)*S). It handles the 4 states: (f_L+a_L, f_L+a_H, f_H+a_L, f_H+a_H)."]
- ['model/household.py', "Implements the `HouseholdSolver` using Numba JIT. Methods include: `solve_labor_supply(w, r, lmbda, tau, k_prime, z)` which uses a bisection to find optimal 'h' from the FOC; `egm_step(...)` which performs one step of the Endogenous Grid Method to find the policy function k'(k, z); and `value_function_iteration` as a fallback."]
- ['model/economy.py', 'Implements the `Economy` class to handle production and government constraints. Includes `get_prices(K, N)` to return (w, r) based on Cobb-Douglas marginal products, and `solve_lambda(Y, g, tau, Γ)` to ensure the government budget is balanced according to the tax function yd = lambda * y^(1-tau).']
- ['solvers/steady_state.py', "Defines `SteadyStateSolver`. It iterates until the capital market and labor market clear. It contains a nested loop: Outer loop guesses 'r' and 'lambda', inner loop solves the household's problem and computes the stationary distribution Γ using an eigenvector method or distribution iteration."]
- ['solvers/transition.py', 'Defines `TransitionSolver`. It implements the algorithm from Appendix A: Guess paths {rt, lambdat}, backward induction for policy functions {kt, ht}, and forward simulation for the distribution {Γt}. It iterates until the paths of prices and fiscal parameters converge. Essential for computing the β_g weighted welfare.']
- ['utils/metrics.py', 'Provides utility functions: `compute_gini(distribution, values)` for inequality; `compute_welfare(V, Γ)` for utilitarian aggregation; and `consumption_equivalent_variation(V_benchmark, V_new)` to quantify welfare gains in percentage consumption units.']
- ['main.py', 'The entry point. It executes three main experiments: 1. Steady-state comparison (Table 2); 2. Search for the optimal τ given β_g (Figure 4); 3. Generation of transition dynamics plots (Figure 3).']

[Shared Knowledge]
The logic for calculating 'lambda' is shared between the steady-state solver and the transition solver; in both cases, the government expenditure 'g' is fixed at the benchmark level (17% of initial Y), and 'lambda' must adjust to balance the budget given the current distribution. Household policies use a non-linear k-grid that is much denser near 0 to capture borrowing constraint effects.

----------------------------------------
[Anything UNCLEAR]
The paper mentioned 'non-monotonic' transfers but used a power function yd = λy^(1-τ). For τ > 0, the tax function is progressive, but we must ensure the bisection for labor supply handles cases where the marginal tax rate might exceed 1 or income is zero. The specific convex combination parameter for updating 'r' and 'lambda' path guesses in the transition solver is not specified; I will use a damping factor of 0.1 to 0.2.
